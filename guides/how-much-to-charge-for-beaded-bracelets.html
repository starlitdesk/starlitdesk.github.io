How much to charge for beaded bracelets, stretch to woven

To price a beaded bracelet, add the beads, cord or clasp and packaging, your minutes at an hourly rate and a small share of running costs, then price so a margin is left after fees. Minutes decide it: in our invented example a 12-minute glass stretch bracelet costs $6.05 and keeps $4.56 at $12.00, a gemstone stretch bracelet keeps only $0.96 at $12.00, and a 90-minute woven seed-bead bracelet costs $34.85 and needs $58.02 for a 30% margin.

1. Cost one bracelet: beads, cord or clasp, packaging, minutes

Count everything that leaves with the bracelet:

  • Beads: what one bracelet uses, from the strand or tube price.
  • Cord or clasp: elastic cord for a stretch bracelet; for a clasped one, the clasp, crimps and any extender chain.
  • Packaging: card and bag.
  • Your time: the minutes from first bead to packed, at an hourly rate you would accept. If you are not sure what that rate is, see how to work out your hourly rate for handmade work.
  • Running costs: a small share per bracelet for tools, glue, needles and fair fees you do not count elsewhere.

true cost = beads + cord or clasp + packaging + minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate + running costs share

break-even price = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %)
price for your margin = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

2. Worked example: glass stretch, gemstone stretch, woven seed-bead

Example figures are invented: your beads, suppliers, times and fees will differ. Labour at $20.00 an hour, an example fee of 9.5% of the price (rounded to the cent) + $0.25 per order (not any platform's rate), a 30% target margin and a running costs share of $0.40 per bracelet, as in our earrings guide. A basic stretch bracelet is cheap to make. TheCraftMap, a craft fair directory, writes: "A basic stretch bracelet on quality elastic cord with glass or acrylic beads might cost you 75 cents to $2 in materials." (How to sell bracelets at craft fairs, 9 Sep 2026). Our glass row, $1.65, sits inside that range. The woven bracelet costs less in beads than the gemstone one, but its 90 minutes make it the dearest by far.

3. Test the prices you are thinking of ($10 to $60)

Keeps = price − fee − true cost, and margin = keeps ÷ price. Per hour = (price − fee − materials − running costs) ÷ hours, so it is what your making time really earns (0.2 hours for a stretch bracelet, 1.5 hours for the woven one).

TheCraftMap writes that "Most handmade bracelets sell in the $10 to $25 range, with the exact spot set by your materials and your crowd." and "Simple stretch and clay bead bracelets usually land at $10 to $15." That is one source's view, not a market rate. In our example the $10 to $15 band clears a 30% margin for the glass bracelet from $10.41, but not for the gemstone bracelet below $16.36, and the woven bracelet needs more than twice the $25 top of the range.

4. Check "materials × 4" against your true cost

A common quick rule is to multiply materials. TheCraftMap gives "materials times two for your wholesale number, then double again for retail" and says: "Price at materials times four as a starting retail number, then confirm it pays your time fairly." Do that check:

BraceletMaterials × 4FeeKeeps
Glass stretch$6.60$0.88−$0.33
Gemstone stretch$21.00$2.25$9.10
Woven seed-bead$17.80$1.94−$18.99

The rule leaves out your time, so it fails worst on slow designs: the woven bracelet at $17.80 against a true cost of $34.85. Even the quick glass bracelet loses $0.33 once the fee is counted. An older beading magazine article suggests "Your cost of materials + anywhere from 25% to 40% for profit" and labour that ranges "anywhere from $5 to $20" an hour (beading magazine, 2011, updated 2015). It does count time; check the hourly rate against your own.

5. Stacks of 3 for Christmas: check them against singles

TheCraftMap's advice is "Sell stacks, not singles." and it gives a "3 for $30" sign next to $12 singles as an example. Run the numbers with your own prices first. Three separate glass sales at $12.00 keep 3 × $4.56 = $13.68. "3 for $30" as one order has a fee of $3.10 and keeps $30.00 − $3.10 − $18.15 = $8.75, or $2.92 a bracelet: $4.93 less than three singles.

A mixed gift stack of 2 glass bracelets and 1 gemstone bracelet costs $21.75. Its 30% floor is ($21.75 + $0.25) ÷ 0.605 = $36.36. At $35.00 it keeps $9.67 (27.63%); at $40.00 it keeps $14.20 (35.50%). Sold singly at $12.00 + $12.00 + $18.00, the three keep $15.51 (the gemstone single at $18.00 has a fee of $1.96 and keeps $6.39). A stack is good only if it turns a one-bracelet sale into three. More on sets in how to price a gift set.

6. Sizes: one stretch size or a clasp with an extender

TheCraftMap writes that "The average adult wrist runs about 6.5 to 7.5 inches", that "A finished length of 7 to 7.5 inches fits most adult women, and 7.5 to 8.5 inches fits most men.", and: "For clasped bracelets, add a two-inch extender chain so one size adjusts across several wrist measurements." If you add an extender, put its cost in the clasp line, as the woven example does; if you make several stretch sizes, each size still needs its own beads and minutes counted.

7. How many bracelets before Christmas

From Mon 26 Oct to Mon 14 Dec 2026 is 49 days, 7 weeks. At 6 making hours a week that is 42 hours: 210 stretch bracelets at 12 minutes, or 28 woven bracelets at 90 minutes. Common questions

How much should I sell beaded bracelets for?

At least your true cost plus fees and the margin you want. In our invented example that is $10.41 for a glass stretch bracelet, $16.36 for a gemstone one and $58.02 for a 90-minute woven bracelet. TheCraftMap's $10 to $25 range is one source's view, not your price.

How much to charge for clay bead bracelets?

Use the stretch method, like the glass row: count the clay beads and cord, time each design and add packaging and running costs.

How much do beaded bracelets sell for?

No source gives a market price, and this guide does not state one. Work from your own cost, then compare with what similar bracelets sell for near you.

Should I sell bracelets in stacks?

Only if the stack makes people buy more. In our example "3 for $30" keeps $4.93 less than three single $12.00 sales of glass bracelets.
