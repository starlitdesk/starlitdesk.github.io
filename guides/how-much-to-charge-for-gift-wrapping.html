How much should you charge for gift wrapping?

Charge at least your cost per wrap divided by 0.905, so a 9.5% payment fee is covered. In our example a paper-and-box wrap costs $3.50 in materials and 6 minutes, so the floor is $3.87, and a $5 add-on keeps $1.02. Shoppers commonly see about $3 to $10, and one survey found more than half would pay $3.99. Free wrap is a choice, not a default: on a $30 order it takes $3.50 of $10.90 profit (32.11%).

1. What one gift wrap really costs

A wrap is a small product. Count every material that goes into it and the minutes it takes. One ecommerce platform's guide lists paper, tissue, ribbon, boxes, cards and labels, and says to "Calculate average wrapping time per tier and multiply it by your fully loaded hourly wage" (ecommerce platform blog, 19 Dec 2025). All inputs below are invented; labour is $15 an hour.

cost per wrap = materials + $15 × minutes ÷ 60

The same guide says to "Add a buffer that accounts for the percentage of wraps that need rework." If 1 wrap in 10 has to be redone, add 10%: the standard wrap below then costs $3.85, not $3.50.

2. The break-even add-on price

When wrap is sold as an add-on, the fixed $0.25 per-order part of the example fee is already paid by the order, so the add-on only pays the 9.5%. The lowest price that covers the wrap is:

break-even = cost per wrap ÷ 0.905

For the standard wrap: $3.50 ÷ 0.905 = $3.87, rounded once. With the 10% redo buffer: $3.85 ÷ 0.905 = $4.25.

4. What shoppers will pay

The platform guide above puts "Typical gift wrapping pricing" at "$3 to $10 depending on service tier and product category". A shipping company's survey of 2,200 US consumers in November 2023 found that more than half would pay $3.99 for gift wrap from a retailer they don't know, and that willingness drops much less between $3.99 and $5.99 than between $5.99 and $7.99 (Pitney Bowes, 20 Dec 2023). An earlier survey by the same company found people pay mainly for skill, not time: "It's not about time, it's about skill and creativity." (Pitney Bowes, 22 Dec 2021). A local gift shop's holiday service charged "$5 to $25 per gift", by number, size and style (Palisades Post, 19 Dec 2024).

These are vendor surveys and one shop's prices, not market rates. We found no sourced figure for how many orders add wrap, so plan with your own numbers.

5. Free gift wrap: what it takes from an order

Take a $30 order with an item cost of $16.00 and a fee of 9.5% + $0.25 per order.

$34 item, free wrap built into the price$3.48$11.02

Free wrap takes $3.50 of $10.90, which is 32.11% of the order's profit. There are two ways to cover it.

Build it into the price. To keep $10.90 with free wrap, the item needs (10.90 + 16.00 + 3.50 + 0.25) ÷ 0.905 = $33.87. Round up to $34 and the order keeps $11.02.

Set a free-wrap threshold. The platform guide suggests: "Offer free basic wrapping once orders hit a target value that sits above your current AOV" (AOV is your average order value). On a $60 two-item order (cost $32.00) the fee is $5.95 and the order keeps $22.05 without wrap or $18.55 with free wrap, so wrap takes 15.87% of the profit instead of 32.11%.

Free wrap is also a holiday offer, like a discount: see Black Friday discount maths for the same kept-per-order test.

Common questions

How much should I charge for Christmas gift wrapping? At least your cost per wrap ÷ 0.905. In the example that is $3.87 for a $3.50 wrap; $5 keeps $1.02. Typical prices run from about $3 to $10.

Should I offer free gift wrapping? Only if the price covers it: either raise the item price (here $34 instead of $30) or offer free wrap above your usual order value.

How much do people pay for gift wrapping? In one 2023 US survey, more than half would pay $3.99, and willingness held up fairly well to $5.99.

Should I charge more for big or awkward items? Yes. They take more minutes and materials, so price them as a higher tier. If the items are sold as a set, see how to price a gift set for packaging cost per set.
