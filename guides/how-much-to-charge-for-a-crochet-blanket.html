How much to charge for a crochet blanket, from baby to king

To work out how much to charge for a crochet blanket, crochet one swatch in the blanket's stitch and yarn, weigh it and time it, then scale both by the blanket's area: that gives the yarn and hours for any size. Add finishing time (border, weaving in ends), cost the yarn by the skeins you buy, pay your hours, and price for fees and margin. In the illustrative example below, a 50 × 60 inch throw takes 30.78 hours and 1,000 g of yarn, and a full-cost price is $820.00; at $250 it pays $5.29 an hour.

1. Why blankets are the hardest crochet item to price

"Blankets are among the most time-intensive crochet projects," as one yarn shop puts it (Secret Yarnery). A crochet app vendor estimates that a 50 × 60 inch throw "typically takes 30-50 hours depending on the stitch pattern and yarn weight" (CraftsTrack). The problem is that hours grow with the blanket's area: a queen is almost three times the size of a throw, so it takes almost three times the work. A single "blanket price" cannot be right for every size.

2. Weigh and time one swatch

Crochet a square swatch in the exact stitch, hook and yarn you will use, at least 6 × 6 inches. Weigh it on a kitchen scale and time it from the first chain. Yarn needs depend on "the finished blanket size, stitch pattern, yarn choice, and your tension" (CY Crochet), so your own swatch beats any chart. Pattern time estimates often run short; time yourself.

3. Scale the swatch to each size

swatches = blanket area ÷ swatch area

yarn = swatches × swatch grams · crochet hours = swatches × swatch minutes ÷ 60

The sizes below are example sizes; check your pattern or your customer's bed.

4. Add finishing hours and cost whole skeins

The border, weaving in ends, blocking and photos are not in the swatch. Add them as finishing hours per size. Then cost the yarn by the skeins you have to buy: if a blanket needs 360 g and skeins are 100 g, you buy 4 skeins. The leftover yarn may be usable later, but this order paid for it.

A yarn multiple pays about $3.23 to $3.47 an hour at every size here, because yarn is a small share of a blanket's cost (throw: $60.00 of $617.04).

The short-hours trap: price the throw on a pattern's 15 hours instead of the measured 30.78 and labour is $270.00, cost $333.00 and the floor ($333.00 + $0.45) ÷ 0.755 = $441.66. The blanket still takes 30.78 hours: after the $42.41 fee and $63.00 of materials, $336.25 is left, or $10.92 an hour. The price is $378.34 lower ($820.00 − $441.66).

Common questions

How much should I charge for a crochet baby blanket? Scale your swatch to the size. In the example a 30 × 36 inch baby blanket takes 11.50 hours and 4 skeins ($24.00); the full-cost price is $315.00, while $150 pays $9.42 an hour.

How much should I charge for a queen size crochet blanket? In the example a 90 × 90 inch queen takes 81.00 hours and 27 skeins, so the full-cost price is $2,155.00. At $500 it pays $3.54 an hour.

How much to charge for a crochet throw blanket? The example throw takes 30.78 hours; the full-cost price is $820.00, and $250 pays $5.29 an hour.

Can I just charge three times the yarn cost? You can, but in the example it pays about $3.30 an hour at every size. Check the per-hour figure first.
