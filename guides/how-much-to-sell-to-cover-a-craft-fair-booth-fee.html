How much do I need to sell to cover a craft fair booth fee?

Selling three times the booth fee only covers your costs if your margin is high. Work out your own break-even from your cost of goods, card fees and the rest of the event costs, then compare deals: a flat fee is cheaper when you sell a lot, a commission is cheaper when you sell little. In the example below, selling 3× a $150 booth fee leaves $110.60 when stock costs 30% of the price, but only $20.60 when it costs 50%.

The short answer: your multiple depends on your margin

The "sell 3×, 7× or 10× the booth fee" rules give one number for every seller. But a seller whose stock costs 30% of the price keeps far more of each sale than one whose stock costs 50%. Two numbers decide whether a fair pays for itself:

  • Your break-even sales: the sales at which the booth fee, the other event costs, the stock you sell and the card fees are all paid.
  • The cheapest deal at your sales level: a flat booth fee, a commission on sales, or a smaller fee plus a commission.

This guide works out both. For the full list of event costs and a slow-day test, see is a craft fair worth it?

Where 3×, 7× and 10× come from

These are rules of thumb passed between sellers, not measured data:

  • 3×: "aim to gross at least 3 times your booth fee", described as a rule many experienced vendors use (TheCraftMap, Feb 2026).
  • 7×: "makers should aim to sell 7 times what they pay for the booth fee", and 10×: a seller's forum post that "about 10x my booth fee is a pretty profitable show" (both quoted by SmartAsset, updated Sep 2023).

For scale, the same TheCraftMap guide estimates booth fees at $25-75 for small community fairs, $75-200 for mid-size regional fairs, $200-500+ for large juried shows and $100-400 for holiday markets. These are vendor estimates; your organiser's price is the one that counts.

Your own break-even multiple

Each dollar of sales leaves you a little less than a dollar after the stock you sold and the card fees. Divide the event costs by what is left:

break-even sales = (booth fee + other costs) ÷ (1 − cost of goods % − card fee % − fixed card fee ÷ average sale)
break-even multiple = break-even sales ÷ booth fee

The same idea is often done per item: the fee divided by the profit on each item tells you how many you must sell (Craft Professional). The formula above also counts card fees and the other costs.

In the example, each dollar of sales leaves 0.668 at 30% cost of goods: 1 − 0.30 − 0.026 − 0.15 ÷ 25. So $190 ÷ 0.668 = $284.43, rounded up to $285.00, 1.90× the booth fee. Check at $285.00: cost of goods $85.50, card fees $7.41 + $1.71 = $9.12, and 285.00 − 85.50 − 9.12 − 190.00 = 0.38.

At 50% cost of goods each dollar leaves 0.468, so $190 ÷ 0.468 = $405.98, rounded up to $406.00, 2.71× the booth fee. Check: 406.00 − 203.00 − 13.00 − 190.00 = 0.00.

So in this example 3× is just above break-even for the 50% seller and a modest profit for the 30% seller. Use the multiple as a floor for your own margin, not as a target.

Flat fee, commission, or both: where the deals cross

Some fairs take a percentage of your sales instead of a flat fee, usually 10-15% according to TheCraftMap's budget guide (Mar 2026), and some organisers charge a lower booth fee plus a small percentage of sales (TheCraftMap, organiser guide). The deals cross at simple points. A and C cost the same at $150 ÷ 0.15 = $1,000.00 of sales; A and B at ($150 − $40) ÷ 0.10 = $1,100.00; B and C at $40 ÷ 0.05 = $800.00. Below $1,000.00 the 15% deal costs less than the flat fee; above $1,100.00 the flat fee is cheapest. At $450 of sales, deal C keeps $82.50 more than the flat fee.

Break-even sales also move: about $284.43 under deal A, $140.85 under B and $77.22 under C (the event costs ÷ what each dollar leaves after the commission). A commission shares the risk of a slow day with the organiser; a flat fee rewards a strong one. TheCraftMap estimates that first-year sellers take $200-800 per event (vendor estimate); in this example, anywhere in that range one of the commission deals costs less than the flat fee.

What to ask the organiser before you pick

  • Is the percentage taken on all sales, or only on card sales the organiser processes?
  • Is there a minimum fee, or a cap?
  • When is the fee due, and what happens if you cancel? Check your vendor agreement; terms differ by organiser.
  • Who takes the payments: you, or the organiser's till? That changes your card fees.

Common questions

Can I realistically 3× the booth fee in sales? Compare 3× with your break-even multiple. If your break-even is above 3×, as it can be with low margins or high travel costs, 3× is not enough.

What percentage of sales should go toward craft fair expenses? There is no single figure. In the example, the $190 of booth and other costs is 42.22% of $450 in sales but 18.10% of $1,050. The more you sell, the smaller the share.

Should I include my time? Yes: divide profit by every hour the fair takes, as in the per-hour column, and compare it with your hourly rate.

How much stock should I bring? See how much inventory to bring to a craft fair. Your cost of goods share comes from your prices: how to price handmade items.
