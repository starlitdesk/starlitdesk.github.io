How to price pottery for wholesale

Price pottery for wholesale from your cost per sellable piece when you make a batch, not from your current retail price. Shops usually pay about half of retail, so first check that half your retail still covers clay, glaze, firing, losses, your time and packing. In our example a mug sold at $19 costs $10.47 to make one at a time, so $9.50 wholesale loses $0.97; made in a batch of 48 it costs $8.17, and a $12.00 wholesale price ($24.00 retail) keeps $3.83 (31.92%).

1. Why half of your retail price may not work

Shops buy at a wholesale price and roughly double it on the shelf. On a potters' forum, one member put it as "The TYPICAL markup (retail price) is 100% over the wholesale price in the art/craft field", and another wrote "My wholesale prices are 50% of my retail prices" (potters' forum thread, read Mon 12 Oct 2026). If your retail price was set for one-at-a-time sales with a margin of about a third, half of it can sit below what the piece costs you. Check before you quote.

2. Cost a piece made in a batch

A wholesale order changes some costs and not others. Clay, glaze, firing and losses per sellable piece stay about the same. Your minutes per piece usually drop, because you throw, trim and glaze the same shape over and over. Packing drops too, when pieces go out in one carton instead of one gift box each. Time a real batch before you rely on a lower figure. One ceramics magazine column is clear that "labor is not profit" (ceramics magazine column): your time is a cost, even in a batch.

3. Set a wholesale floor, then double for retail

Your floor is the lowest wholesale price that still leaves the margin you want. Wholesale is usually paid by invoice, so in this guide there is no platform fee on it (an assumption; if you sell through a wholesale platform, take its commission off first).

wholesale floor = batch cost per piece ÷ (1 − target margin)
retail = wholesale × 2

The same column works a cost of $19.25 up to $38.50 wholesale and $77 retail by doubling twice. Doubling your cost for wholesale is the safest rule; the floor is the least you should accept.

Is $24.00 a believable retail price? One production mug pottery's wholesale page says "The suggested retail price is between $19.95 and $29.95" (wholesale page, read Mon 12 Oct 2026). $24.00 sits inside that band; $32.68 sits above it. The shop's own shelf prices decide what retail will bear. Raising your own retail price to match is fine; selling to shops below what you charge yourself is what undercuts you.

5. Kiln loads for one order

To deliver 48 good mugs at a 10% loss rate, make 54 (48 ÷ 0.9 = 53.33, rounded up). With our pottery guide's 30 mugs per bisque load and 24 per glaze load, that is 2 bisque loads and 3 glaze loads, 72 glaze spaces for 54 mugs: 18 spare. The batch cost assumes full kilns, so fill those 18 spaces with stock for your own shop. A half-empty load raises the firing cost per mug, as our pottery guide shows.

Time: 54 mugs × 14 minutes = 12.6 hours of making for one order. The cost table, like our pottery guide, spreads losses over clay, glaze and firing only, so it pays for 48 × 14 minutes = 11.2 hours. If your loss rate is high, add the extra time too.

6. Minimum order and lead time before the holidays

Minimums and lead times are normal. A wholesale potter on a pottery podcast says "I just ask for a $250 minimum" and "in our busy season, which is spring and fall, we say 8 weeks lead time" (podcast episode, 24 Jun 2025). The production pottery above sets "The minimum per design is 48 pieces for any of our signature mugs" and quotes "8-9 weeks from order approval".

One order of 48 mugs at $12.00 is a $576.00 invoice and keeps 48 × 3.83 = $183.84. A $250 minimum at $12.00 a mug is 250 ÷ 12 = 20.83, so 21 mugs.

With an 8-week lead time, an order agreed on Mon 12 Oct 2026 is delivered Mon 7 Dec; one agreed on Mon 19 Oct, Mon 14 Dec. For a shop that wants stock by Mon 30 Nov, the order had to be agreed by Mon 5 Oct at 8 weeks, or by Mon 19 Oct at 6 weeks.

Shipping to the shop is quoted separately: "Shipping costs should be calculated separately", as the magazine column puts it. One mug maker's FAQ says "it's around +$1/mug for shipping costs, but it varies depending on your location" (FAQ).

7. Wholesale or consignment?

An Australian ceramics association calls an agreed wholesale price "The cleanest method", calls consignment "a riskier proposition from your perspective", and says gallery "Commissions are generally 25% – 60% of the sale price" (association article). On consignment you are paid only when a piece sells; see how to keep track of consignment stock. Common questions

How do I price pottery for wholesale? Cost one piece as you make it in a batch, divide by 0.70 for a 30% margin, round up and double for retail. In the example a batch mug costs $8.17, its floor is $11.67, and $12.00 wholesale with $24.00 retail keeps $3.83 a mug.

What is a normal wholesale pottery price? Usually about half of the retail price, but the right number is the one above your own batch cost floor. In the example that is $11.67 for a mug, rounded to $12.00.

What should a ceramic wholesale price include? Clay, glaze, both firings, losses, your minutes in the batch, packing and overhead. Shipping is quoted on top.

How much should I charge for my pottery? For your own shop, see how to price pottery; for shops, check the wholesale floor here first and set your retail price from it.
