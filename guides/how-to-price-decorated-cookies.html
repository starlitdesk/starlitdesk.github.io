How to price decorated cookies per dozen: decorating time sets the price

To price decorated cookies per dozen, add up ingredients, packaging and overhead, then add your baking, decorating and packing time at an hourly rate, and price so a margin is left after fees. Time is the big number: in the illustrative example below, ingredients and oven are $4.20 of a $47.70 basic dozen, and an elaborate dozen costs $95.70 because it takes 4.25 hours. Labour is 78-89% of the cost, so the design level, not the dough, sets the price.

Why decorated cookies need their own price

Ask on a baking forum what to charge for decorated cookies and you get answers like "between $12 and $120 a dozen (or more)" (CakeCentral, 2013). The range is that wide because the ingredients barely change between a one-colour cookie and a seven-colour one, while the time does. One cookie business platform says a simple single-colour cookie "might take a couple of minutes to decorate", an intricate one "fifteen or twenty", and that a dozen intricate cookies "can be four or five hours of piping, flooding, drying, and detailing" (Homegrown). A decorator on a cookie forum allows "approximately 15 minutes per cookie from when I start decorating to when I finish" (Cookie Connection, 2013). That is why the method below costs each design level on its own.

A common way to name the levels comes from a cookie blogger: basic uses "1-2 colors and consistencies of icing", detailed "4-6 icing colors and consistencies", and elaborate "7 or more colors of icing" (Sweet Sugarbelle, 2012). Use whatever levels match your own designs.

Step 1: cost the batch and split it per dozen

Cost the dough, the royal icing, colours and sprinkles, and the oven for one batch, then divide by the dozens you can actually sell. Count broken and test cookies as lost: the ones you sell carry their cost. The per-gram method for ingredients is in how to price cookies and baked goods; this guide does not repeat it.

Step 2: add packaging per dozen

Individual bags and a box add up quickly on decorated cookies. One reply on the blog above puts individual bags at "about twenty-five cents per cookie" (Sweet Sugarbelle, reader reply, 2019). Price your own from the pack you buy.

Step 3: time yourself

Time three parts separately: prep (mixing dough, baking, cooling, mixing and colouring icing) for the whole batch, decorating per cookie at each design level, and packing per dozen. Prep is shared across the batch; decorating is not, so a dozen always carries 12 cookies' worth of decorating.

Check common rules against your hourly pay

Three rules of thumb come up again and again. A craft fair guide says "decorated sugar cookies usually run $3 to $6 apiece" and that a boxed dozen of assorted cookies "commonly lands between $18 and $40" (TheCraftMap, Sep 2026). Forum posters often suggest "$1 per inch for decorated sugar/gingerbread cookies" (CakeCentral, 2009). Here is what two of those prices pay per hour in the example, after the fee and the $10.70 of costs before time:

Fee = price × 3% + $0.30. Left = price − fee − $10.70. Per hour = left ÷ total hours for that level.

The same guide also says to "charge at least three to four times your ingredient cost", and more for anything hand-decorated. In the example the ingredients alone (without the oven) are 12.40 ÷ 3 = $4.13 a dozen; four times that is $16.52, or $1.38 a cookie, which is below the $47.70 cost of even a basic dozen once packaging, overhead and time are in. That is why the "more for hand-decorated" part matters.

Quote elaborate and custom sets on their own

Elaborate and custom designs vary the most, so time a test cookie of each new design before you quote, and price the dozen from that time. A mixed tray is easiest as one line per type per dozen, then the tray. For mixed holiday boxes, see how to price a gift set; for the rate itself, see how to work out your hourly rate.

Common questions

How long should it take to decorate a cookie? Anything from a couple of minutes for a simple design to 15-20 minutes for an intricate one, according to the sources above. Time your own designs and use those minutes, not someone else's.

Should I charge per cookie or per dozen? Work out a dozen first, because prep and packing are shared, then divide for a per-cookie figure. Quoting per dozen with a minimum order keeps small orders from costing you time.

Is there a market for $10 cookies? We cannot tell you that. The arithmetic says an elaborate dozen in the example costs $95.70, which is $7.98 a cookie at $20 an hour, before any fee or margin.

How do I price a cookie tray? Price each cookie type per dozen on its own line, then add the tray and any extra packing time.
