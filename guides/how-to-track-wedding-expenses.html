How to track wedding expenses: planned vs actual

To track wedding expenses, give every category a planned amount, replace it with the latest real figure as estimates turn into quotes, contracts and final bills, and watch the difference against a buffer you set aside at the start. In the illustrative example below, a $24,000.00 budget keeps $1,200.00 (5%) as a buffer; seven categories end up $1,070.00 over plan, which uses 89.17% of the buffer and leaves $130.00.

Why a list of payments is not enough

A list of what you have paid tells you where the money went. It does not tell you whether the wedding still fits the budget, because most of the cost is agreed long before it is paid. Tracking expenses means comparing two numbers per category: what you planned, and what that category will really cost now. For how much is due when, see the companion guide wedding vendor payment schedule; this guide is about the totals.

Step 1: set the budget, then take the buffer off first

Write the total you can spend at the top. Before you split it into categories, set part of it aside for things that cost more than planned. The Knot suggests keeping 5% of the budget as a "just in case" fund, and its 2026 Real Weddings Study (10,474 US couples married in 2025) puts the average wedding at $34,200 (The Knot, updated 24 Jun 2026). An Irish wedding site gives a wider range: "Set aside a contingency fund (usually 5–10% of your total budget) for unexpected costs." (weddingsonline.ie, 1 Aug 2025). These are rules of thumb from those sites; the size of your buffer is your choice.

In the example, 5% of $24,000.00 is $1,200.00, so $22,800.00 is split across the categories.

Step 2: one line per category, with the source of each figure

Give each category three things: the planned amount, the latest figure, and where that figure comes from. Figures firm up in a fixed order:

  • Estimate: your own guess, or a price list.
  • Quote: a written price from the vendor for your date and numbers.
  • Contract: the amount you signed for.
  • Final bill: what the vendor charged in the end, for example after the final head count.

The same Irish guide says: "As you receive actual quotes from suppliers, update your figures to reflect the real prices." Writing the source next to the figure shows at a glance which lines can still move.

Step 3: "actual" is the agreed cost, not what you have paid

The latest figure is the full amount you are committed to, paid or not. Wedding website maker Wedsites puts it this way in its help pages: "If a budget item has payments, the actual cost may be calculated from the total of paid and unpaid amounts." (Wedsites Help). In the example, a 30% venue deposit of $2,520.00 has been paid, but the venue line counts the whole $8,400.00 contract.

Step 4: test the lines that can still move

Catering is quoted per guest, so the final head count changes it. If 118 guests come instead of 110, catering becomes 118 × $65.00 = $7,670.00, another $520.00. The categories would then be $1,590.00 over plan, $390.00 more than the whole buffer. Seeing that before the head count is due gives you time to decide what to change, for example a smaller order for stationery and favours, which is still an estimate.

Step 5: keep it current

  • Update a line the day a quote, contract or bill arrives, and change its source.
  • Itemise: one line per vendor or purchase, so a small overspend does not hide inside a big category.
  • Keep every receipt and contract in one folder, digital or paper.
  • Check the buffer line after every change; it is the one number that tells you whether the plan still holds.

Common questions

What is the best way to track wedding expenses? One table with a line per category or vendor, a planned amount, the latest figure and its source, and a buffer line at the bottom. A spreadsheet adds it up for you; paper works too if you re-add after each change.

How much buffer should we keep? The sources above suggest 5% (The Knot) or 5–10% (weddingsonline.ie). Those are rules of thumb; pick a figure and track it.

Should tips and small purchases go in? Yes. Give them their own lines; small items are where budgets drift.

Do we count a cost when we sign or when we pay? When you sign or accept a quote. Payments are tracked separately, by due date.
