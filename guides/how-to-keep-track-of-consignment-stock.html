How do I keep track of consignment stock and payouts?

Once a month, for each shop, write down what you dropped off, what sold, what the shop owes you at your agreed share, and what it has paid. The gap between owed and paid is your payout check; the pieces left over, counted on the shelf, are your stock check. In the worked example below, two shops look fine at first glance, but one still owes $25.00, one piece is missing ($15.00 of your share), and the slower shop has sold only 25.00% of what you left.

The short answer: one row per shop, five numbers, once a month

Consignment means a shop displays your work and pays you a share only when a piece sells. Most shops pay monthly, with a report of what sold (TheCraftMap). That report is the moment to check. For each shop, keep five numbers:

  • Dropped off: pieces you have delivered and not taken back.
  • Sold: pieces on the shop's sales report.
  • Owed: sales at retail × your share.
  • Paid: what actually reached your account.
  • Counted: pieces you can see on the shelf.

Two checks follow from them: still owed = owed − paid and missing = dropped off − sold − counted. If both are zero, the shop's report and your records agree.

What to record at drop-off

Give every piece a code or SKU and keep a running log of what you left at each shop (TheCraftMap). One vendor guide suggests asking the shop owner to sign the inventory list at each drop-off and pickup (Made Urban), so both sides start from the same list. Record per piece:

  • shop, item code, description and retail price;
  • drop-off date;
  • your agreed share for that shop.

Splits differ by shop, so keep the share per shop, not one figure for everything. A 60/40 split in the maker's favour is the figure most often quoted (Craft Professional), and one vendor guide puts the usual range at 50-70% to the maker (TheCraftMap). The split maths per channel is in our guide to wholesale vs retail pricing.

When the shop reports sales: owed against paid

your share = pieces sold × retail price × your share %
still owed = your share − paid to you

Do this per shop, from the shop's own report, as soon as the payment arrives. A gap is not always a problem: a payment may be in transit or cover only part of the month. But a gap that carries over to a second month is worth a friendly question.

Count the shelf: dropped off minus sold

should be on the shelf = dropped off − sold
missing = should be on the shelf − counted

Count when you visit, or ask the shop for a count. A missing piece was either sold and not reported, or lost, damaged or stolen. While your stock sits in a shop you carry more risk if a piece goes missing, so loss and damage belong in your agreement (Made Urban). Value a missing piece at your share of its retail price: that is what you would have been paid had it sold.

Sell-through and slow stock

sell-through = sold ÷ dropped off × 100

Sell-through shows which shops actually move your work. A low figure is a reason to swap the pieces for different ones, or to collect them. Unsold stock comes back to you at the end of the agreed term (TheCraftMap), but no standard term exists. Our own rule of thumb, not an industry standard: anything that has been out for more than 90 days gets swapped or collected.

Sales = sold × retail. Share = sales × share %. Shop keeps = sales − share. Still owed = share − paid. Sell-through = sold ÷ dropped off, to two decimals. Missing = should be on the shelf − counted; its value = pieces × retail × share %. Stock out = counted × retail.

Shop A sold 7 pieces, $175.00 at retail, so your 60% is $105.00. It paid $80.00, so $25.00 is still owed. It should have 12 − 7 = 5 pieces left, but you count 4: one piece is missing, worth 1 × $25.00 × 60% = $15.00 of your share. Ask about both in one message: perhaps the missing piece sold and the $25.00 is part of its payment, perhaps not.

Shop B has paid in full, but it has sold only 5 of 20 pieces, a sell-through of 25.00%, against 58.33% at Shop A. Six of its 15 remaining pieces have been out for more than 90 days, so swap or collect them. Across both shops, $370.00 of your work at retail is still on other people's shelves; count it as stock you own, for example when you plan how much holiday stock to make.

What your agreement should state, so the numbers can be checked

This is not legal advice, and we do not supply an agreement. For the monthly check to work, you need to know from each shop: the split, when and how it pays, how sales are reported, how long it keeps stock, how unsold stock comes back, and who pays for lost or damaged pieces. If any of these is unclear, the numbers above cannot settle a difference.

Common questions

How do I track inventory on consignment? Log every piece you drop off with a code, price and date; record each shop's sales report and each payment; once a month compare owed with paid and count the shelf against dropped off minus sold.

What is a typical consignment split for handmade items? 60/40 in the maker's favour is the most quoted figure, with 50-70% to the maker as a common range. See wholesale vs retail pricing for what each split keeps you per item.

Do you get paid upfront when selling on consignment? No. You are paid only when a piece sells (Craft Professional), usually monthly.

What happens if a consignment shop doesn't sell my items? Unsold pieces come back to you at the end of the agreed term. Check sell-through monthly so you can swap slow pieces before then.
