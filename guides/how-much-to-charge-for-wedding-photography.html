How much to charge for wedding photography

Price a wedding package from every hour it takes, not just the hours on the day. Count the planning calls, travel, the coverage itself, culling and editing, and delivery; multiply by your cost per hour; add direct costs such as a second shooter; then add the card fee and a margin. In the worked example below an 8-hour wedding is 38 hours of work and needs at least $2,039.75 just to cover costs and pay; at $2,800.00 it keeps a 26.36% margin.

The formula

hours worked = planning + travel + coverage + cull and edit + delivery
package cost = cost per hour × hours worked + direct costs
break-even price = (package cost + fixed card fees) ÷ (1 − card fee %)

Your cost per hour comes from a year of costs (your pay, running costs, gear spread over its life) divided by the hours you work on clients. Our guide on how to price a photography session works it out step by step. Because your pay is inside that hourly cost, the break-even price already pays you; profit comes on top.

Step 1: count the hours behind one wedding

Illustrative figures only; every input is invented. We use the same invented photographer as the session guide: a year of costs of $48,000.00 over 1,200 client hours, so $40.00 per hour. Time your own weddings and use your numbers.

The example counts, for each package: 2 hours of planning (enquiry, consultation and a timeline call), 2 hours of travel, the hours of coverage, 3 hours of culling and editing for every hour of coverage, and 2 hours to deliver the gallery. So hours worked = 6 + 4 × coverage hours: an 8-hour wedding is 6 + 4 × 8 = 38 hours of work.

Step 2: the break-even price, with card fees built in

Direct costs per wedding: a second shooter at $50.00 per hour of coverage, $36.00 mileage, $15.00 online gallery and $9.00 backup storage. Clients pay in two card payments (a retainer and the balance), each at an assumed 2.9% + $0.30, so the fixed part is $0.60 per wedding; use your own provider's rates.

Time cost = hours worked × $40.00. Direct costs = $50.00 × coverage hours + $60.00. Break-even = (cost + $0.60) ÷ 0.971. Fees = price × 2.9%, rounded to the cent, + $0.60. Price at 20% margin = (cost + $0.60) ÷ 0.771.

For the 8-hour package: 38 × $40.00 = $1,520.00, plus $460.00 direct costs = $1,980.00. Break-even is $1,980.60 ÷ 0.971 = $2,039.75; the fees on that are $59.75, and $2,039.75 − $59.75 = $1,980.00, so the price covers the cost and your pay exactly, with nothing left over.

You keep = price − fees. Profit over cost = you keep − cost from the table above. Margin = profit ÷ price. Per coverage hour = price ÷ coverage hours; per hour worked = price ÷ hours worked.

On the day, the 8-hour wedding looks like $350.00 an hour. Spread over the 38 hours it really takes, it is $73.68 an hour before costs. That is why a package priced from coverage hours alone can sit below break-even without anyone noticing.

Step 4: price the extra hour

Couples often ask for one more hour on the day. In this example each extra hour of coverage brings 3 more hours of editing, so it is 4 hours of work: 4 × $40.00 = $160.00, plus $50.00 for the second shooter = $210.00 cost. It is paid with the balance, so only the 2.9% applies: break-even is $210.00 ÷ 0.971 = $216.27, and a 20% margin needs $210.00 ÷ 0.771 = $272.37. At $275.00 the fee is $7.98 and the extra hour clears $57.02 over its cost.

Step 5: retainer and balance

Many photographers take part of the price when the date is booked and the rest before the wedding. In the example the couple books the 8-hour package at $2,800.00 with a 30% retainer of $840.00, and the balance of $1,960.00 is due 30 days before a wedding on Sat 5 Jun 2027, which is Thu 6 May 2027. The fees on the two payments are $24.66 and $57.14, together $81.80: the same as in the table. How much to take up front, and whether it is refundable, is your choice and depends on your contract; our guide on how much deposit to take on a custom order shows the arithmetic, and the couple's side of the same schedule is in wedding vendor payment schedule.

Common questions

How much should I charge for wedding photography? At least the break-even price for each package: cost per hour × hours worked (planning, travel, coverage, editing, delivery), plus direct costs and card fees, then a margin on top.

Should I price by the hour or by package? Either works for the couple, as long as the number comes from hours worked. A package price that is coverage hours × an hourly rate leaves out the editing and planning.

Is a second shooter worth it? In the example the second shooter is $400.00 of the 8-hour package's $1,980.00 cost. If you add one, add their cost to the package; if a couple asks to drop one, take that cost out.

What if local prices are lower than my break-even? Offer fewer coverage hours or fewer edited images rather than a price below your floor; re-run the sum for the shorter package.
