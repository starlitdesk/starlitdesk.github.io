How to organize beads for jewelry making

To organize beads for jewelry making, sort them by type first and by size or colour second, keep metal findings apart from glass beads, give every type its own labelled pocket and work from a small project tray instead of the whole stash. Count your types before you buy or print storage: in our example, 30 bead types and 8 findings types need 38 pockets, which is 4 trays of 12 pockets with 10 pockets spare.

Beads rarely get lost because there are too many of them. They get lost because a bag of mixed beads has no fixed place, so you cannot see what you own and you buy the same colour twice. This guide covers the sorting order, the labels, the work tray and how many pockets your stash needs. For storage across a whole craft room, shelf planning and printing tips, see how to organize a craft room with 3D-printed storage.

1. Decide how you look for beads

A jewelry-making educator answering a reader's question about a large bead inventory names three things to settle first: how much space you have, whether you start a design from a colour or from a material, and "the big one, how much product do you have?" (Fire Mountain Gems, Ask the Experts). The second point decides your sort order. If you start from a material ("I want to use these Czech glass rounds"), the material is your first level. If you start from a colour ("something in teal"), colour comes right after type, so all your teals sit side by side.

2. Sort by type, then by size or colour

A bead retailer's storage guide puts it this way: "If your stash is mixed, use a layered system: material or bead type first, then color or size." (The Bead Obsession, 29 Jun 2026). In practice:

  • Level 1, type: seed beads, glass rounds, gemstone chips, metal spacers, wooden beads.
  • Level 2, size or colour: whichever you search by. Seed beads are usually easier by size, because a pattern calls for one size; larger beads are usually easier by colour.
  • One type per pocket. A pocket with two types is a mixed bag again.

Size the pocket to the bead. Small seed beads and findings sit well in pockets of about 25 mm; buttons and larger beads need 30 mm or more.

3. Keep findings apart from glass

The same retailer advises: "Metal spacers, clasps, and charms should usually be stored separately from glass beads." Give findings their own tray: jump rings, crimps, clasps, ear wires, head pins and spacers. Findings are what you reach for on every piece, whatever its colours, so one findings tray that sits next to you all the time saves the most searching.

4. Label every pocket

A label saves you from opening and guessing. The bead retailer's minimum is: "At minimum, include the bead type, size, and color family." Add the finish (matte, opaque, AB) if you stock the same colour in two finishes. A short label is enough, for example "Seed 11/0 · teal · matte". Put the label where you can read it with the tray in place: on the front edge, or on a strip of tape along the row of pockets.

Keep the stash visible. A furniture maker's guide to jewelry supplies says: "The cardinal rule for small items is: if you can't see it, you'll forget you have it." (Create Room, 25 Feb 2026). Open shallow trays on a shelf, or in a shallow drawer, show every colour at once.

5. Work from a project tray, not from the stash

Before you start a piece, move only what that piece needs into a small tray. The bead retailer: "Pull only the beads you need into trays, mats, or small cups while you are designing." The Create Room guide suggests a tray that holds "only the beads, findings, and wires for your current piece". When the piece is done, put the leftovers back in their pockets before you start the next one. That one habit keeps the main stash sorted.

Plan a short reset now and then. Create Room suggests setting aside 30 minutes every few months for what it calls a "Creative Reset". Use it to empty the project tray, re-sort strays and update labels for new types.

Worked example: how many trays for 38 types

Illustrative stash; count your own types. Tray sizes are the ready sizes of the bead sorting tray in our pack (pocket grid and outer size from the model file).

Count types, not beads: one pocket per type. This stash has 30 bead types and 8 findings types, 38 types in all. With 12 pockets per medium tray, 38 ÷ 12 = 3.17, so it needs 4 trays (48 pockets), which leaves 10 pockets spare for new types. Keeping the findings apart, the 30 bead types fill 3 trays (36 pockets, 6 spare) and the 8 findings types fill 1 tray (12 pockets, 4 spare).

Option A is the simplest. Option B leaves room for a growing stash but needs a deeper shelf. Option C gives the findings smaller 25 mm pockets, which suit jump rings and crimps, for 34.8 mm more width. A spare small tray also makes a good project tray.

Common questions

What is the best way to store beads? In shallow trays or boxes with one type per pocket, sorted by type and then size or colour, with findings in their own tray and a label on every pocket. The best system is the one you can put things back into quickly.

Should I organize beads by colour? If you design from colour, yes, but within each type: all teal seed beads together, all teal glass rounds together. Mixing types in one colour pocket makes it hard to find the right size.

Can the trays be stacked? The trays in our pack have flat tops and bottoms but no stacking or nesting feature, and we have not tested stacking them. Side by side on a shelf, or in a drawer, is the safe choice.

Does the pack's label holder fit the trays? Not in its ready sizes: they are taller than the tray fronts. Use tape or a paper label on the trays; the label holder is meant for the bins.
