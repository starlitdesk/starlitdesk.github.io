How to make a wedding budget: start with the guest count

To make a wedding budget, write down the total, set a buffer aside, then split every cost into two kinds: fixed costs that stay the same whatever the guest count (venue hire, photographer, music) and per-guest costs that grow with every name on the list (food, drinks, cake, invitations). The money left after fixed costs, divided by the cost per guest, tells you how many guests the budget fits. In the illustrative example below, a $25,000.00 budget with $16,100.00 of fixed costs and $100.00 per guest fits 76 guests.

Step 1: write the total, then take the buffer off

Start with the one number you can actually decide: the total you can spend. Before you plan anything else, set part of it aside for costs that come in higher than planned. The Knot advises: "Try to earmark at least 5% of your budget for unforeseen costs." (The Knot budget split, updated 29 Sep 2026). That is a rule of thumb; the size of your buffer is your choice. In the example, the budget is $25,000.00 and the buffer is 5%: $1,250.00. That leaves $23,750.00 to spend.

Step 2: sort every cost into fixed or per guest

Go through each thing you plan to pay for and ask one question: does it cost more if one more guest comes? The venue hire, the photographer and the DJ usually cost the same for 60 guests or 120. Food and drinks do not: The Knot notes, "There's a per-head cost for food and liquor, and these two are among the biggest expenses in a typical wedding budget." A wedding planning and registry company puts it more broadly: "Your guest count directly affects most wedding expenses, making it one of your biggest budget decisions." (wedding cost guide, updated 6 Mar 2026).

Some costs sit in between (a venue that charges per head, flowers per table, more tables to hire). Put them where the quote puts them: per guest if the price is per head, fixed if it is one price.

Step 3: how many guests fit

Take the fixed costs off the money you have to spend, then divide what is left by the cost per guest:

guests that fit = (to spend − fixed costs) ÷ cost per guest

In the example: (23,750.00 − 16,100.00) ÷ 100.00 = 76.5, so 76 guests. Round down: half a guest does not fit.

Step 4: what the guest count does to the total and the cost per guest

The total for any list is the fixed costs plus the per-guest cost times the number of guests:

The cost per guest falls as the list grows, from $368.33 at 60 guests to $234.17 at 120, because the fixed costs are shared by more people. The total still rises by $100.00 with every guest. That is why a published average cost per guest is not a budget on its own: The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study puts the average at $292 per guest, with 117 guests and $34,200 in total (The Knot average wedding cost, updated 29 Sep 2026), and your own figure moves with your list.

Step 5: check against published percentage splits

Percentage splits are a useful check, not a plan, because they disagree. The Knot's split gives venue and rentals 29% and catering, cake and drinks 24%. Step 6: if the list is longer than the budget

Say you want 100 guests. At $100.00 a head the total is $26,100.00, $2,350.00 over the $23,750.00 to spend. There are two levers:

  • Lower the cost per guest. To fit 100 guests, it must fall to (23,750.00 − 16,100.00) ÷ 100 = $76.50, which is $23.50 less per guest.
  • Lower the fixed costs by $2,350.00, for example a cheaper venue hire or a shorter photography booking.

Or keep the list at 76. Using the buffer to cover a longer list leaves nothing for the costs that come in higher later.

Common questions

How do you do a wedding budget? Write the total, take a buffer off, sort every cost into fixed or per guest, and work out how many guests fit, as in steps 1 to 3.

What is the wedding budget breakdown by percentage? The Knot's split gives venue and rentals 29%, catering, cake and drinks 24%, photo and video 10% and flowers and decor 9%. A wedding website company's split puts the reception venue at 18 to 22% instead, so use any split as a check, not a plan.

What is the average wedding cost per guest? The Knot's 2026 study puts it at $292. The wedding cost guide above says "Budget approximately $150-300 per guest for a typical wedding, though this varies by location and service level." Your own figure depends on your list size, because fixed costs are shared by every guest.

How do I make a wedding budget spreadsheet? Make two blocks, fixed costs and per-guest costs, and one cell for the guest count. Total = fixed total + per-guest total × guests. Then track planned against actual figures as quotes arrive.
