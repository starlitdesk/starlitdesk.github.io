How much should I charge for engraving tumblers?

To price an engraved tumbler, add the blank, the box, every minute you spend (artwork, setup, engraving, cleaning) at your hourly rate, a little machine time and running costs, then price so you keep a margin after fees. In our worked example one name tumbler costs $14.02. It breaks even at $15.77, keeps $8.35 (33.40%) at $25.00, and loses $0.70 at $15.00. Price a second side, customer-supplied cups and bulk orders separately.

The short answer: blank, minutes, fees, then margin

Engraved tumblers are one of the most asked-about laser jobs, and the quoted prices vary widely: vendor guides put a basic name or logo at $15 to $25 (Rabbit Laser USA, Apr 2025) and custom tumblers at $25 to $50 (The Maker's Chest, Sep 2025). A range cannot tell you where your own price should sit. Your costs can:

  • True cost: blank, box, your minutes, machine time and a share of running costs.
  • Break-even price: the price at which a sale pays that cost and the selling fees.
  • Your price: break-even plus the margin you want to keep.

Step 1: cost one tumbler

Count the minutes, not just the engraving time. Artwork, lining up the cup, cleaning and packing take longer than the laser itself on a simple name. One vendor guide times a basic job at about 5 to 8 minutes of setup and engraving (Rabbit Laser USA); add your own artwork and packing on top. The example counts every minute you are at the machine, including while it runs, at the hourly rate from how to work out your hourly rate. The machine rate of $0.865 an hour is the example rate from how to price laser engraving.

Step 2: break-even and a 30% margin price

The example uses an invented fee of 9.5% of the order plus $0.25 per order, the same example rate as our ornament guide; use your own.

break-even price = (true cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee %)
price for a target margin = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % − margin %)

Break-even: (14.02 + 0.25) ÷ 0.905 = $15.77. Check: fees on $15.77 are $1.50 + $0.25 = $1.75, and 15.77 − 14.02 − 1.75 = $0.00. For a 30% margin: (14.02 + 0.25) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = $23.59, so $25.00 is a reasonable single price.

Fees = price × 9.5%, rounded to the cent, + $0.25. Margin = price − fees − $14.02 true cost. Earned per hour at the laser = (price − fees − blank, box, machine and running costs) ÷ 16 minutes × 60: what each hour of your time earns before you pay yourself.

At $15.00 each hour at the laser earns $17.36, less than the $20.00 you set, and the sale loses money once your time is paid. At $25.00 it earns $51.30.

Step 4: price the second side

Re-aligning takes 3 minutes and engraving 6 more, so the second side adds $3.00 of labour and $0.09 of machine time: $3.09. At $25.00 + $8.00 = $33.00, the tumbler costs $17.11, fees are $3.39 and it keeps $12.50 (37.88%), $4.15 more than one side at $25.00. Charge for the extra minutes, not a token amount.

Step 5: the customer brings the tumbler

When a customer supplies the cup, you sell only your time: engraving only costs $5.33 labour + $0.09 machine + $0.50 running costs = $5.92.

Vendor guides quote bulk tumblers at $10 to $18 each (Rabbit Laser USA); at this blank price, the bottom of that range would lose money. If you buy blanks by the case, one price list says cases of 25 or more often cost 30 to 50% less per unit (Craftstrack); a cheaper blank is what makes a low bulk price work. Rules of thumb you will see

  • Per minute: The Maker's Chest quotes $1 to $3 per minute of machine time. For the example: 16 minutes × $1.00 = $16.00, only $0.23 above our $15.77 break-even. The low end only just covers cost at this blank price.
  • Blank times five: the same guide says a $5 blank should retail for $25 to $30 at least. Applied to our $7.50 blank × 5 = $37.50, above most quoted single prices; use it as a ceiling test only.
  • Minimum charge: $15 to $25 (Craftstrack) or $25 to $30 (The Maker's Chest). A minimum stops small jobs from losing money once setup is counted.

One laser maker's product round-up lists a 70-85% profit margin on tumblers from the blank cost and selling price alone, with no breakdown for labour or fees (Aeon Laser). Counting every minute and the fees, as above, is what tells you whether a price pays you.

Common questions

How much do people charge to engrave tumblers? Vendor guides quote $15 to $25 for a basic name and $25 to $50 for custom work. Check your own break-even first: in the example it is $15.77.

How much should I charge for laser engraving tumblers? At least break-even after fees, and usually enough for a margin of around 30% on top of paying your time: $25.00 in the example.

How much should I charge if the customer brings their own tumbler? Your time, machine and running costs, plus margin: $15.00 keeps $7.40 in the example. Agree who replaces a ruined cup first.

Should bulk tumbler orders cost less per cup? They can, because setup is shared, but cost the whole order first: shared setup saved $1.78 a tumbler in the example, not $5.

How much extra for engraving both sides? Cost the extra minutes. In the example a second side costs $3.09 and an $8.00 charge adds $4.15 to the margin.
