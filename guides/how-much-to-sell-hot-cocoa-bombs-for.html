How much should you sell hot cocoa bombs for?

Price a hot cocoa bomb from the whole batch: chocolate, cocoa mix, marshmallows, molds, the shells that crack and your time, then add the box. In our example a bomb costs $3.70 to make and $5.28 boxed; to keep 30% after a 3% + $0.30 fee you need at least $8.33, so $9 keeps $3.15. One food-seller software company puts handmade bombs at about $5 to $15 each and gift sets at $12 to $25. Check those ranges against your own cost before you copy them: in our example a $25 box of 6 pays you $14.95 an hour, not $18.

1. What goes into one bomb

A bomb is two chocolate half-shells filled with cocoa mix and marshmallows, sealed and decorated. Published recipes use 12 ounces of chocolate for 6 bombs, or about 2 ounces each: one lists "12 ounces high-quality bittersweet chocolate" with "Yield: 6 servings" in "2 large 6-cavity silicone hot chocolate bomb molds (2 1/2-inch diameter cavities)" (recipe), and a second uses 12 ounces of melting wafers for 6 (recipe). The example below uses 2 oz of chocolate, 0.5 oz of cocoa mix, 0.25 oz of mini marshmallows and $0.10 of decoration per bomb. These amounts and all times are our example inputs; the pack prices are list prices we read on 17 October 2026.

2. Cost a batch of 24 and count the cracked shells

Shells crack when you unmold them or warm the edges to seal. In the example you start 26 bombs and sell 24, so the 24 carry the ingredients of all 26. The batch method, from pack price to cost per unit, is the same as in how to price cookies and baked goods.

Of the $3.70, ingredients are $1.63 and your time is $1.88. The chocolate is not the big cost; your time is.

3. Molds and your time per batch

A 6-cavity hemisphere silicone mold lists at $14.99 at a baking supply retailer (mold). Four molds cost $59.96; spread over 300 bombs in a season that is $0.20 a bomb. Count the mold as a cost even though you already own it: it wears out, and next season you buy it again.

Time is melting, two coats, setting, unmolding, filling, sealing and decorating. The example allows 2.5 hours of hands-on time for 24 bombs, about 6.25 minutes each, paid at $18 an hour. Time your own batch; the setting time in between does not count if you do other work while it sets.

Fee = price × 3% + $0.30, rounded once to the cent. Keeps = price − fee − cost. Margin = keeps ÷ price. Your time is already paid inside the cost, so what an order keeps is on top of $18 an hour.

With no margin at all, a single breaks even at (5.28 + 0.30) ÷ 0.97 = $5.75.

5. Why a box of 6 at $25 pays you less than you think

The same company says "Price gift sets as the hero" and puts sets at $12 to $25 (Homegrown, 25 September 2026). In our example a box of 6 costs $26.24, so $25 loses $2.29 even before any margin. The box holds $13.38 of your labour (6 × $1.88 + $2.10) for 44.5 minutes of work (6 × 6.25 + 7). Take away the $2.29 loss and you are paid $11.09 for those minutes: $14.95 an hour, not $18. A box of 6 needs $39.61 to keep 30%; at $40 it keeps $12.26.

6. A 24-piece favour order

Weddings, offices and parties sometimes ask for 24 at once. One batch makes them, each in a single box, as one order:

The favour order costs 24 × ($3.70 + $0.48 + $0.60) + $0.50 = $115.22, because the order fee and overhead are paid once. It keeps less in total than 24 single sales but sells the whole batch in one go. For discount tiers on bigger orders, see how to price a bulk order of handmade items.

7. Better chocolate: what it adds per bomb

A maker's store lists two 2 lb bags of melting chocolate wafers at $53.34 for 64 oz (wafers). The batch's 52 oz then cost $43.34 instead of $24.28: $19.06 more a batch, or $0.79 more per sellable bomb. Fancy decoration adds in the same way as icing time on decorated cookies: count the minutes.

8. Shortcuts that lose money

"Ingredients × 3" gives 3 × $1.63 = $4.89, below the $5.28 a boxed single costs before any fee. "$5 each" loses $0.73 an order in the example. Both leave out your time, the box and the fee per order.

9. Before the season

The same source says "The season runs from roughly Thanksgiving through Valentine's Day and peaks in December". Re-cost before you take orders, and set a last order date: when to stop taking custom Christmas orders works back from shipping dates. If you ship, note that "Chocolate begins to soften around 72 degrees" (Homegrown): cold packs and insulated mailers are a cost per order, so add them to the box line.

Common questions

How much do hot cocoa bombs sell for? One vendor gives about $5 to $15 each and $12 to $25 for gift sets. In our example the 30% floor for a boxed single is $8.33.

How much should I charge for a box of 6? In the example at least $39.61 to keep 30%. At $25 the box pays $14.95 an hour.

Is $5 a bomb enough? Not in our example: a single $5 order loses $0.73.

Does better chocolate change the price? In the example it adds $0.79 a bomb; decoration adds whatever your extra minutes cost.
