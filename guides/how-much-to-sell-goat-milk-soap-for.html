How much to sell goat milk soap for: cost the milk, then price

To price goat milk soap, cost the batch as you would any cold process soap, add the milk as its own line (powder, bought milk, or your own milk at $0 cash), add any extra prep minutes, then price so a margin is left after fees. In our example the milk adds only $0.20 a bar, yet the bar needs at least $10.38 for a 30% margin; at the common $8.00 it keeps $0.96.

1. Cost the milk as its own batch line

Goat milk replaces some or all of the water in a batch, so it is one more ingredient line with a weight and a price. Work it out per ounce, like every other line:

milk cost per batch = milk used × price per ounce

One soap supplier lists powdered goat milk at "4 oz $8.99", "1 lb $21.99" and "5 lbs $93.99", with the mixing line "Combine 1 oz. of powdered milk with 8 oz. warm distilled water." (supplier listing, read Mon 19 Oct 2026; prices change). The 5 lb bag works out at $93.99 ÷ 80 oz = $1.1749 an ounce and the 1 lb bag at $21.99 ÷ 16 oz = $1.3744: small bags cost more per ounce. Bought fresh milk is priced by the quart or litre, so turn it into a price per ounce first. If you milk your own goats, the cash cost is $0, but the minutes are not (section 4).

Count the extra minutes too: thawing, mixing or keeping milk cold adds prep time to the batch. This guide gives no recipe, lye or milk-handling guidance; take the weights and method from your own recipe.

3. Test the prices you see at markets ($8, $10, $12)

The same blog suggests "Price a cold process goat milk soap bar at $8 to $10 when it cures to 4.5 ounces." and says "Eight small goat farms we checked charge a median of $7.51 for a 4 to 5 ounce bar, with prices from $7.00 to $10.50." That is one source's view, not a market rate. Test those prices against your own cost. Fee = 9.5% of the price, rounded to the cent, + $0.45. Keeps = price − fee − cost, and margin = keeps ÷ price.

4. Your own herd's milk: $0 cash is not $0 time

Milk from your own goats costs nothing at the till, but the 15 extra minutes still cost $0.50 a bar at $18.00 an hour (15 × $18.00 ÷ 60 ÷ 9). In the example the own-milk bar costs $6.13, more than the powder bar's $5.83, and its floor is $10.88. If you would rather not count those minutes, you are paying for them out of your hourly rate; see how to work out your hourly rate for handmade work.

5. Does "double your cost" work?

The blog's method is to "Price a goat milk soap bar by working out your full cost, doubling it". Applied to the full cost in our example:

Both clear the 30% floor here because doubling the full cost leaves room for a 9.5% fee. With a higher fee or a smaller margin target the same rule can fall short, so check it against your floor rather than using it as the price.

7. When to pour for December markets

Cold process bars need weeks to cure before you can sell them. As our melt and pour soap guide notes, cold process "takes about 4-6 weeks to cure", so for a market on Fri 11 Dec pour by Fri 13 Nov for a 4-week cure, or by Fri 30 Oct for 6 weeks. For delivery by Christmas in the lower 48 states, USPS lists a recommended send-by date of "Thursday, Dec. 17" for Ground Advantage (USPS holiday shipping dates, read Mon 19 Oct 2026). Common questions

How much does homemade goat milk soap sell for?

No source gives a market price, and this guide does not state one. One vendor blog suggests $8 to $10 for a 4.5 oz bar and reports a $7.51 median from eight small farms. In our invented example the floor is $10.38 with powder and $11.16 with bought milk; compare your own floor with prices near you.

Is goat milk soap more expensive to make?

Only slightly per bar in our example: $0.20 more with powder from a 5 lb bag, $0.67 more with bought milk and 15 extra minutes.

Is selling goat milk soap profitable?

That depends on your price. In our example the powder bar keeps $0.96 at $8.00, $2.77 at $10.00 and $4.58 at $12.00.

How much to charge for a goat milk soap gift set?

Price the set from three bars' cost plus any box: in our example three powder bars need at least $29.65 for a 30% margin.
