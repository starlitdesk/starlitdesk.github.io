How to price a bulk or wholesale order of handmade items

To price a bulk order, work out what one piece costs at that quantity, then check any discount against a margin floor. A big order can take a discount because your setup and packing time is spread over more pieces. In the worked example one candle made to order costs $23.20, but at 50 candles the cost falls to $7.52 each, so a 20% bulk discount still keeps 63.10% of the order. The same 20% off on a single candle would lose money.

Why a big order costs less per piece

Some time is spent once per order, whatever the size: getting materials out, setting up, cleaning, labels, packing. Spread over 50 pieces it almost disappears; on one piece it is most of the cost. One maker's guide on batch work puts it this way: making 10 pairs in a batch "wastes far less time during setup" than making them one by one, but pauses between steps can "magnify your labor costs" (Maker's Row). Time your own batch rather than guessing.

Step 2: test your discount tiers

There is no standard bulk discount. One discount-app vendor's example runs from no discount for 1 to 50 units to "10% off" for 51 to 100 and "20% off" for 101 to 200, and notes "Many stores aim for post-discount margins of 30% or more"; it also warns "Don't offer deep discounts on low-margin SKUs." (Qikify, 25 Sep 2025). Another vendor's tier tool starts with 5% steps and says "Compare the output against unit cost before publishing tiers." (Bushe.co). The example tests a $28.00 retail price with 10% off at 10 units and 20% off at 50, paid by card on an invoice at 3.3% + $0.30, the rate Square's US pricing page lists for online and invoice card payments on its Free plan (read Fri 16 Oct 2026).

Fee = order total × 3.3%, rounded to the cent, + $0.30. Kept over cost = order total − fee − order cost.

The single candle keeps only $3.58 at full price. At 50 units, 20% off still keeps $706.74 over cost. If the same 20% were given on one candle, $22.40 minus the fee would not cover its $23.20 cost.

Check at $35.23: the fee is $1.46, which leaves $33.77, or $10.57 over the $23.20 cost: 30.00%. When you quote a per-unit floor, round up to the next cent: 10 × $13.24 = $132.40, just above $132.38.

Your 10% and 20% tiers sit far above these floors, so they are safe in this example. A one-off at $28.00 is the problem: it needs $35.23 to keep 30%.

Step 4: set a minimum order

A minimum keeps small "bulk" requests from costing you more than they pay. Common structures include an order value such as $150 or $200, a unit minimum such as 6 per product, or a minimum that is "higher for first orders, lower for reorders"; one vendor suggests setting it at "the point at which the order is profitable after packing, shipping, and admin time" (Craftybase, 4 Apr 2026). Tie it to your batch size: "If you make candles in batches of 12, a MOQ of 12 or 24 makes sense." and "Dollar-based MOQs are often easier for retailers to understand and hit." (Craftybase, 9 May 2022). In the example, 10 candles at the 10% tier is a $252.00 order and keeps 61.66%.

Step 5: wholesale (half of retail) vs a bulk discount

A shop that resells your work usually wants a wholesale price. One rule of thumb is "COGS × 2 = Wholesale price" and "COGS × 4 = Retail price", with keystone meaning "the retailer buys at 50% of the retail price and sells at full retail." (Craftybase, 4 Apr 2026). Another guide says wholesale buyers "typically expect to pay about half of the retail price of your product." (Craft Professional, 23 Apr 2025). At the example's 50-unit cost, $7.52 × 2 = $15.04 for wholesale, a little above half of retail.

Take a deposit that at least covers materials (see how much deposit to take), and check the hours fit before you say yes: when to stop taking custom Christmas orders and how much holiday stock to make.

Common questions

How much discount should I give for a bulk order? Only as much as your cost per unit at that quantity allows. In the example 20% off at 50 units keeps 63.10%, and the deepest discount that still keeps 30% is 59.71%.

Should a single custom piece cost more than my bulk price? Yes. In the example one candle costs $23.20 and needs $35.23 for a 30% margin, because it carries the whole setup and packing time.

Is wholesale always half of retail? No, it is a common rule of thumb, not a rule. Check it against your own cost per unit, as in Step 5.

What minimum order should I set? One at which the order is profitable after packing and admin, ideally a full batch. Many makers use an order value.
