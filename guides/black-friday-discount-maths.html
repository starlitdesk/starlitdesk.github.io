How much can I discount for Black Friday and still profit?

Your deepest safe Black Friday discount is the one where the sale price still covers materials, packaging, your time and the selling fees on the lower price. Below that floor, every sale loses money. Above it, check the sales multiplier: on a $30 item that earns $8.90 a sale, a 20% discount leaves $3.47, so you need about 2.56 times as many sales to earn the same. This guide shows how to find both numbers for your own items, with two worked tables.

The short answer: a floor price and a sales multiplier

Two numbers decide whether a discount is worth it:

  • The floor price: the lowest price at which a sale still pays your true cost and the selling fees. Never go below it.
  • The sales multiplier: how many times as many sales you need at the discounted price to earn what you earn at full price. Small discounts need a few extra sales; deep ones need many.

Step 1: know your true cost per item

True cost is materials, packaging, your labour and a share of overhead such as tools, workspace and software (Stocksmith). If you have not priced your time yet, start with how to work out your hourly rate, then the full method in how to price handmade items.

Step 2: fees come off the discounted price

Most selling and payment fees are a percentage of the sale plus a fixed amount per order. When the price drops, the percentage part drops a little too, but the fixed part and your costs do not. That is why your margin falls faster than your price.

Step 3: find your floor price

floor price = (true cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee %)
deepest discount = 1 − floor price ÷ full price

The floor still pays your time, because your time is in the true cost. It does not leave the business anything for slow months, so treat it as a limit, not a target.

Step 4: how many more sales each discount needs

sales multiplier = margin per sale at full price ÷ margin per sale at the discounted price

The same method is used by ecommerce break-even calculators, which note that it ignores fixed costs, returns and the effect of price on demand (Wayflyer). It cannot tell you whether a discount will bring the extra sales; it tells you how many it needs.

Worked example: a $30 candle

Illustrative figures only. The true cost of $18.00 includes materials, packaging and time. The fee of 9.5% of the sale price plus $0.25 per order is an invented example, not any platform's rate: use your own.

Floor price: (18.00 + 0.25) ÷ 0.905 = $20.17. Check: fees on $20.17 are $1.92 + $0.25 = $2.17, and 20.17 − 18.00 − 2.17 = $0.00. The deepest discount is 1 − 20.17 ÷ 30.00 = 32.77%.

Ten full-price sales earn $89.00. At 10% off you need 15 sales to earn as much; at 20% off, 26; at 30% off, 119. Small discounts are cheap; deep ones rarely pay, because nearly all the margin is gone.

A second example: $12 earrings

Alternatives to a deep discount

These are ideas to test, not proven results:

  • A small discount of 10-15%, which can stay profitable when margins are healthy (Stocksmith).
  • A set or bundle at a lower price per item: one order fee and one round of packing are spread over several items. Check its margin with how to price a gift set.
  • A small extra such as gift wrap, priced into your cost like any other material.

Discounts are popular with small businesses: in a survey of 508 US small business owners for Xero, 72% said discounts had been their most effective tactic in past holiday sales, and 34% had never run a holiday promotion (Researchscape, Oct 2025). Common questions

How much discount should I offer as a product business? No more than your deepest safe discount, and usually much less: check the multiplier for each step and ask whether you can realistically make and sell that many more.

How do I calculate my margin for Black Friday sales? Sale price minus true cost minus the fees on the sale price, as in the tables above.

Should product businesses do Black Friday? It is a choice. Some makers run a small offer; others keep their prices. The floor and multiplier show what a discount costs you before you decide.
