How much to charge for embroidering a name on a stocking

To price an embroidered name, cost the stitches as machine minutes, add hooping, thread, stabilizer and the blank, then price for your margin after fees. In our example a 6-letter name on a $6.80 stocking costs $11.55 and needs $19.83 to keep 30%; an 11-letter name costs $13.47, so a flat price up to 8 letters plus $1.00 per extra letter keeps the margin.

1. Stitches per letter become minutes

A name is a design whose stitch count grows with every letter. Check the stitch count of your font at the size you sell; in this example a 2.5-inch script letter is about 700 stitches. Then use the method from how to price machine embroidery: stitches divided by your machine's real average speed, plus time for each colour change. We do not repeat that method here; the worked example below uses the same speed, thread cost, labour rate and overhead as that guide.

Stitch only fonts and designs you own or are licensed to use.

2. Hooping a stocking takes longer

A stocking is a narrow tube with a cuff, so it is slower to hoop than a flat tote. Time a few: in our example hooping and unhooping a stocking takes 4 minutes, and 5 minutes for a stocking a customer brings, because you check and handle it more carefully. Hooping is labour, so it goes into the labour minutes, not the run minutes.

4. Price up to 8 letters, then per letter

A flat $20.00 keeps 30% only on short names: on an 11-letter name it drops to 20.90%. A price for names up to 8 letters, plus a charge per extra letter, keeps the margin as names get longer. In our example each extra letter needs ($23.01 − $21.11) ÷ 3 = $0.63 more, so $1.00 per extra letter covers it. Machine Embroidery Geek suggests a similar shape: "set a flat fee for a name (up to 8 characters) then price by the size", adding "$1.50 for each letter for names over 8 letters" for 2-3 inch names (How to charge for embroidery, a blog, updated 31 Oct 2025). Use your own stitch counts to set the add-on.

5. When the customer brings the stocking

With the customer's own stocking there is no blank to buy, so the blank is $0.00, but hooping takes longer. In our example the cost is $5.05 and the price for 30% is $9.09. Many embroiderers quote this kind of job with a hooping fee and a stitch rate instead. Machine Embroidery Geek writes that "Most professionals start with a flat hooping fee anywhere between $5.00 and $8.00" and that "It is quite common for practitioners to charge an extra $1.00 per 1,000 stitches." For 4,200 stitches, counted as 5 thousand, that is $5.00 + 5 × $1.00 = $10.00. At $10.00 our example keeps $3.55 after a $1.40 fee, a 35.50% margin. An older cost guide from Fash (September 2023) gives "$1 to $3 per 1,000 stitches, plus a minimum base fee or setup charge of $5 to $10 per design"; treat both as vendor figures, not market rates.

This example does not put a price on the risk of spoiling a customer's item. Decide how you handle that before you take the job, and say it in your order terms.

6. Family orders

Families often order several stockings at once. Five 8-letter stockings at $22.00 come to $110.00 in one order, so the fixed fee is paid once: fee $10.45 + $0.45 = $10.90, cost 5 × $12.32 = $61.60, and the order keeps $37.50, a 34.09% margin. A family set does not need a discount to be worth more to you than five single orders; if you do offer one, check it the way how to price a gift set shows.

7. How many can you stitch before the ship-by date?

Names on stockings are a Christmas job, so time is the limit. From Mon 2 Nov to Wed 16 Dec 2026 there are 33 weekdays. At 1.5 hours of stitching a day that is 49.5 hours. An 8-letter stocking takes 12.50 labour minutes plus 3 minutes to pack, 15.50 minutes in all, so you can finish 191 stockings before the USPS Ground Advantage send-by date of Thu 17 Dec (USPS lists "Thursday, Dec. 17" for Ground Advantage and "Friday, Dec. 18" for Priority Mail in the lower 48 states: USPS holiday shipping dates). Take off any weekdays you will not stitch, such as Thanksgiving. For when to stop taking orders, see when to stop taking custom Christmas orders.

Common questions

How much should I charge for embroidery of a name?

Cost it from the stitches, hooping, thread, stabilizer and blank, then price for your margin after fees. In our invented example a 6-letter name on a stocking needs $19.83 to keep 30%.

Should I charge per letter?

A price up to a set number of letters plus a charge per extra letter keeps long names from eating your margin. Work the add-on out from your own stitch counts; in our example it is $0.63 a letter, so $1.00 covers it.

What about embroidering caps or other items?

Caps run slower and take longer to hoop: see how much to charge for embroidered hats.
