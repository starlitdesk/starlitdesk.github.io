How to track net 30 invoices on handmade wholesale orders

Net 30 means the shop pays the full invoice within 30 calendar days of the date your terms name, usually the invoice date. To track it, give each invoice one row: date, amount, terms, due date, date paid. Each week, sort by due date and check anything past due. In the illustrative example below, $888.00 is invoiced to four shops in November and $312.00 is still owed on 31 December, 19 days late.

1. What net 30 means

Net 30 gives the shop 30 calendar days to pay the full balance, weekends and bank holidays included, and it normally starts from the date on your invoice (Sage). "Net" means the whole amount, with no discount unless your terms offer one. Shops that buy wholesale often ask for terms like this, and some ask for longer: one wholesale guide notes that suppliers often prefer net 30 while retailers often expect net 60 or net 90 (The Folklore).

The cost to you is time: you have made, packed and shipped the stock, and the money arrives a month later. As one guide puts it, you won't recoup the cost of producing a product until 30 days after you've sold it (Shopify blog).

2. Which date the 30 days count from

Sources disagree. Most count from the invoice date (Sage), but terms can also be based on the date of sale, delivery or completion of work (FreshBooks), and one wholesale glossary defines net 30 as payment 30 days after delivery (CreativeLive). The same invoice can therefore have two due dates. The fix is simple: write the start date on the invoice, for example "Terms: net 30 from invoice date", and print the due date next to it. Then you and the shop are counting from the same day.

Also agree what happens when a due date falls on a weekend. Net 30 counts calendar days, so a due date can be a Saturday; some sellers treat the next working day as on time. Whatever you choose, apply it to every shop.

3. One row per invoice

A tracker does not need to be clever. Record six things for every invoice:

  • shop and invoice number;
  • invoice date (or the start date your terms name);
  • amount;
  • terms, such as net 30, 2/10 net 30 or prepaid;
  • due date: start date + terms days;
  • date paid and amount received.

Record the payment the day the money arrives. A list that is a week behind is the main reason invoices slip.

5. The weekly check

Once a week, sort open invoices by due date, oldest first. For each one past its due date, work out the days late: today − due date. In the example, Shop C was due on Saturday 12 December, so on Thursday 31 December it is 19 days late.

The start date matters here. If Shop C's terms had said "30 days from delivery" and the parcel arrived on Thursday 19 November, the due date would be Saturday 19 December and Shop C would be 12 days late, not 19. Same invoice, two answers: that is why the start date goes on the invoice.

One seller in a Shopify Community thread plans cash as if net 30 invoices are paid at 45 days. That is one seller's practice, not a rule, but in the example it would put Shop A's and Shop B's money on Thursday 17 December and Shop C's on Sunday 27 December.

6. 2/10 net 30: what the early-payment discount costs

With 2/10 net 30, the shop can take 2% off if it pays the full amount within 10 days of the invoice date; otherwise the full amount is due in 30 (Sage). In the example, Shop B paid on day 10, Thursday 12 November, instead of day 30, Wednesday 2 December: $3.72 off a $186.00 invoice, paid 20 days sooner. That is the arithmetic; whether faster cash is worth $3.72 to you is your call.

7. A reminder calendar

A simple cadence, suggested in a Shopify Community post: one list, oldest first, checked weekly; a reminder three days before the due date, one on the due date, then one every seven days. For Shop C's $312.00 invoice:

If a reminder date falls on a weekend, send it on the Friday before. Another seller in the first thread suggests asking each shop when it runs payments and timing reminders a few days before that.

8. New shops: prepaid first, then terms

Sellers in the same thread report starting new shops on prepayment for the first two or three orders, and asking for the accounts contact and purchase order number before offering terms. That is reported practice, not advice. Your own records are the best guide later: a shop that has paid its last few invoices on time is a different risk from one that is regularly 19 days late.

Common questions

What does net 30 mean on an invoice? The full amount is due within 30 calendar days of the start date your terms name, usually the invoice date. Print both the start date and the due date.

How do I track unpaid net 30 wholesale invoices? One row per invoice with date, amount, terms, due date and date paid; check the list once a week, oldest due date first.

Do I need an app? At small volume a spreadsheet is enough. The forum poster above suggests looking at software at around 10-15 open invoices; that is one person's view.

Should a new shop get net 30 straight away? Many sellers ask for the first order or two to be prepaid. It is your decision; we do not give credit advice.
