How much should I charge for handmade earrings?

To work out how much to charge for handmade earrings, cost one pair from a batch: materials, ear wires and backing card, your minutes at an hourly rate and a share of running costs. Then price so a margin is left after fees. In the worked example below, a clay pair costs $4.55 and keeps $11.49 at $18.00, but a beaded pair that takes 30 minutes costs $13.60 and keeps only $2.44 at the same price. The price has to follow your minutes, not the look of the pair.

1. Cost one pair from a batch

Earrings are usually made in batches: you condition and cut a sheet of clay, bake a tray, then sand and assemble. Cost the whole batch and divide by the pairs you can sell, or cost one pair from what it uses. Count everything that leaves with the pair:

  • Materials: clay and finish, or beads and thread, per pair.
  • Findings: ear wires or posts, backs and jump rings. They are small, but for clay earrings they can cost almost as much as the clay.
  • Packaging: backing card and bag.
  • Your time: the minutes per pair, from the first cut to the card, at an hourly rate you would accept. If you are not sure what that rate is, see how to work out your hourly rate for handmade work.
  • Running costs: a small share per pair for blades, sandpaper, oven power, tools and fair fees you do not count elsewhere.

true cost per pair = materials + findings + packaging + minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate + running costs share

2. Find the break-even and a margin price

A payment fee, usually a percentage plus a fixed amount per order, comes out of every price. Price so that what is left covers your cost and the margin you want:

break-even price = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %)
price for your margin = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

4. Why the same $18 works for one pair and not the other

Both pairs look like $18 earrings on a table. The difference is time: the beaded pair takes four times as long, and at $20.00 an hour that is $7.50 more labour per pair. Materials differ by only $1.55. If your designs vary, cost each one; do not give a slow design the same price as a quick one because they sit side by side.

5. Check the rules of thumb against your cost

Earring makers share some quick rules. TheCraftMap, a craft fair directory, writes that "Most handmade polymer clay earrings sell for $15 to $35 a pair at craft fairs." and "For craft fairs, multiply your fully-loaded cost by 2.5 to 4 times." (How to sell polymer clay earrings at craft fairs, 8 May 2026). It also says a pair "costs roughly $1.50 to $3.50 in raw materials" and that "most makers can produce 8 to 12 finished pairs per hour"; our clay example sits inside both ranges.

Applied to our pairs: clay × 2.5 to × 4 is $11.38 to $18.20, beaded is $34.00 to $54.40. The multiplier is a useful second opinion, but only if the cost it multiplies includes your time. Treat the price range as background for clay at fairs, not as your price, and not as a range for slow beaded work.

6. "3 for" deals at a fair: check them against singles

TheCraftMap suggests bundles such as "3 pairs for $40 when individual pairs are $18." Run the numbers with your own prices first. Three separate clay sales at $18.00 keep 3 × $11.49 = $34.47. "3 for $45" as one order has a fee of $4.28 + $0.25 = $4.53, and keeps $45.00 − $4.53 − $13.65 = $26.82, or $8.94 a pair: $7.65 less than three singles. That is fine if the deal turns a one-pair sale into three; it costs you if the buyer would have bought three anyway. The same "3 for $45" with beaded pairs keeps −$0.33: a loss. Common questions

How much should I charge for homemade earrings?

Start from your true cost per pair, including your time, and price for your margin after fees. In our invented example that is at least $7.93 for a quick clay pair and $22.89 for a 30-minute beaded pair. Then compare with what similar pairs sell for near you.

How much to charge for clay earrings?

Clay pairs are quick when made in batches, so their cost is low: $4.55 in our example. Many sell well above the floor, so the question is what your buyers pay. TheCraftMap's fair range of $15 to $35 is one vendor's figure, not a market rate.

How much to charge for beaded earrings?

Time each design. At 30 minutes a pair and $20.00 an hour, labour alone is $10.00, so our beaded pair needs $22.89 for a 30% margin and loses money at $15.00.

Is "3 for" a good deal at a craft fair?

Only if it makes people buy more. In our example "3 for $45" keeps $7.65 less than three single $18.00 sales of clay pairs.
