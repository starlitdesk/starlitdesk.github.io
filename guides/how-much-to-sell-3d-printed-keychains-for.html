How much should you sell 3D printed keychains for?

Price a 3D printed keychain from its full cost, not its grams. Printed 20 to a plate, a keychain in our example costs $1.40: $0.25 of filament, power and machine time, $0.75 of your time, and $0.40 for the ring and card. To keep 30% after a 9.5% + $0.45 fee you need at least $3.06; at $5 you keep $2.67. Vendor guides put typical keychain prices at $3 to $15, more for custom names. Sets of three and name keychains carry the fixed order fee best.

1. What one keychain really costs

A keychain is a small part, so cost a whole plate and divide. This uses the same method and example rates as how to price 3D prints: filament at $20 per 1,000 g, machine wear at $0.30 per print hour, 5% failed prints and labour at $18 an hour. All inputs are invented except the power figures in section 2.

The plastic itself is about 17 cents a keychain ($3.20 ÷ 20 ÷ 0.95). A 3D print service's blog says the same: "Most small keychains cost just a few cents in filament" (JLC3DP, 28 Aug 2025). The ring costs more than the plastic. Bought by the hundred, split rings list at about 25 to 37 cents each in local money at two retailers (Canada, UK); the example uses $0.30. A premium filament at about $36 a kilogram instead of $20 adds about 13 cents a keychain.

2. Why power is the smallest line

One printer maker's own table puts a small printer's average draw printing PLA at "80w" (printer maker's power table). The US government's forecast for the average 2026 home electricity price is 18.21 cents per kWh (EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026). A 5-hour plate uses 5 × 0.080 = 0.4 kWh, which is about 7 cents for all 20 keychains. Use your own printer's draw and your own rate; the line stays small.

3. The price floor and three price points

The fee here is an invented example of 9.5% of the price plus $0.45 per order, not any platform's rate. The lowest price that keeps a 30% margin after it:

floor = (cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

For one keychain: (1.40 + 0.45) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = $3.06.

Fee = price × 9.5% + $0.45, rounded once to the cent. Keeps = price − fee − cost. Margin = keeps ÷ price.

At $3 the fee takes $0.74, more than half of what the keychain cost to make, and the sale falls just under the 30% target. The fixed part of the fee is the same on a $3 keychain as on a $30 order.

6. Name keychains: price the design minutes

A custom name adds design time: say 10 minutes at $18 an hour, $3.00. The cost becomes $4.40 and the 30% floor (4.40 + 0.45) ÷ 0.605 = $8.02.

7. Why per-gram pricing fails for keychains

At $0.10 a gram, an 8 g keychain is $0.80. The fee is $0.53 and the cost $1.40, so each sale loses $1.13. Grams measure the cheapest line on the page; the ring, your minutes and the fee per order are what a keychain price has to cover.

8. Designs you may sell

Print only models you designed or whose licence allows selling prints. A model under a Creative Commons NC licence does not: the deed says "You may not use the material for commercial purposes." (Creative Commons BY-NC 4.0). Logos, characters and team names need the owner's permission.

Common questions

How much do 3D printed keychains sell for? Vendor guides give about $3 to $15. In our example the 30% floor is $3.06 and $5 keeps $2.67.

How much should I charge for a custom name keychain? Add the design minutes to the cost. Here the floor is $8.02 and $10 keeps $4.20.

Should I price keychains by the gram? No. In the example $0.80 by weight loses $1.13 a sale.

Can I sell keychains from downloaded models? Only if the licence allows commercial use. NC licences do not.
