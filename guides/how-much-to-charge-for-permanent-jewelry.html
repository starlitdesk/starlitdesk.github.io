How much to charge for permanent jewelry: chain, time and booth fee

Charge for the chain you use, by the inch, plus a fixed amount that covers your jump ring, welding gas, your 10 to 15 minutes in the chair and your share of the booth fee. A flat bracelet price works until the anklet, the large wrist or the quiet market day: check those three before you print a price sign. In our worked example a gold-filled bracelet costs $26.89 and keeps 65.11% at $85.00, but the anklet at the same price keeps only 58.93%.

What permanent jewelry artists charge

"Most permanent jewelry artists charge by the inch," says a welder maker's blog, where artists on its panel quote sterling silver and gold-filled bracelets "anywhere from $50 to $150", "$50 to $100 for a dainty bracelet" and solid gold "between $115 to $300 or more" (Sunstone, Mar 2024; vendor panel, not a market survey). Those ranges tell you what some artists ask, not what your own chain, time and booth cost. The steps below build your price from your own numbers.

1. Cost the chain by the inch, including the bit you trim

Chain is bought by the foot or by the inch. One supplier lists a 1.3 mm sterling rope chain at $1.25 an inch and a 0.9 mm 14k yellow gold rope chain at $11.10 an inch (Riva Precision, list prices read Sun 11 Oct 2026; prices move). Divide your foot price by 12, then cost the length you actually cut: the fitted length plus the bit you trim off. Our example adds 0.5 inch of trim to every piece.

In the example: $6.00 a foot ÷ 12 = $0.50 an inch for sterling silver, and $21.00 a foot ÷ 12 = $1.75 an inch for gold-filled.

2. Add the jump ring and welding gas per weld

Each piece takes one jump ring in the chain's metal (example: $0.35 sterling, $0.90 gold-filled) and a little argon. Divide a fill by the welds you get from it: $90.00 ÷ 250 welds = $0.36 a weld. A bookkeeping blog works the same way with its own figures, "Argon at $80 per fill over ~200 welds is $0.40 per weld" (Beancount.io, Aug 2026; a blog example, not a market rate).

3. Count your chair minutes, not just the weld

"The actual join takes a second or two," but a craft-fair guide says to "plan on roughly ten to fifteen minutes per customer", and "two people together is closer to half an hour" (TheCraftMap, Jul 2026). Fitting, choosing the chain, cutting and taking payment are all your time. At the rate from how to work out your hourly rate, the example counts 15 minutes at $20.00 an hour = $5.00 a customer.

4. Spread the booth fee over the customers you expect

At a market, the booth fee is part of every piece you sell there. Divide it by a realistic number of customers, not your best day: $120.00 ÷ 16 customers = $7.50 each. For more on booth fees, see how much to sell to cover a craft fair booth fee.

5. Worked example: sterling and gold-filled bracelets

Cost = chain + jump ring + gas + time + booth share. The example card fee is 2.9% of the sale + $0.30, rounded to the cent (invented; use your own). Keep = price − fee − cost, and margin = keep ÷ price. The 60% floor is the lowest price that still keeps 60%: (cost + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.029 − 0.60).

6. Flat price or per-inch price?

A flat bracelet price is simple to show on a sign, but the chain cost grows with every inch. The anklet uses 10.5 inches of chain, so it costs $32.14 and keeps 58.93% at the same $85.00: below the 60% line. Its 60% floor is $87.44, against $73.29 for the standard bracelet.

A per-inch price fixes that. The example charges $45.00 plus $4.50 for every inch of gold-filled chain used:

7. The quiet market day

Six customers instead of sixteen puts $20.00 of booth fee on each bracelet. The gold-filled bracelet then costs $39.39, keeps $42.84 (50.40%) at $85.00, and its 60% floor jumps to $106.98. Some markets now have "three permanent jewelry vendors" (TheCraftMap), so a slow day is worth planning for. Price for a realistic day, and check what a quiet one does before you book the booth; is a craft fair worth it? shows the full event test.

8. How many bracelets pay for the welder

The same welder maker says its top welders "start at $999" and that you "should be able to recoup your investment after selling 50 to 70 bracelets" (Sunstone; a vendor rule of thumb). Work it out from what you keep, not the price: an invented $1,200.00 welder ÷ $45.85 kept per sterling bracelet = 26.17, so the 27th bracelet pays it off. Our example pays back faster than the vendor's rule because its price is high relative to the chain cost; with dearer chain or a lower price you need more.

Questions sellers ask

How much do people charge for permanent jewelry? Artists on one welder maker's panel quote $50 to $150 for sterling or gold-filled bracelets and $115 to $300 or more for solid gold (Sunstone). Check where your own cost puts you with the table above.

How much can you charge for permanent jewelry? As much as your customers will pay, but never less than your floor: cost plus fees plus the margin you need. In the example, the gold-filled bracelet's 60% floor is $73.29.

How much does it cost to make permanent jewelry? In the example, $16.96 for a sterling bracelet and $26.89 for a gold-filled one, with time and booth share included. A bookkeeping blog costs its own example chain at "$18 per foot is $1.50 per inch" (Beancount.io).
