How much to sell beeswax candles for: tapers, pillars, jars

To price a beeswax candle, cost the wax by weight, add the wick, packaging, your minutes and overhead, then price so a margin is left after fees. Beeswax is the expensive line: in our invented example the wax in an 8 oz jar costs $4.61 against $1.43 for soy, so the jar needs at least $16.26, a pair of 10-inch tapers $15.92 and a 3 × 4 inch pillar $25.75 for a 30% margin. Sold at a soy price, a beeswax candle keeps far less.

1. Cost beeswax by weight, not by the candle

Weigh the wax that goes into one finished candle, then multiply by what an ounce of your wax costs. One candle supplier lists yellow beeswax at "1 lb Bag ($12.20 /lb) $12.20", "5 lb Bag ($10.84 /lb) $54.21" and "6+ Bags ($10.54 /lb) $52.72" (supplier listing, read Mon 19 Oct 2026; prices change). A 5 lb bag works out at $54.21 ÷ 80 oz = $0.6776 an ounce. The soy wax in our 4 oz and 8 oz candle guide costs $0.2311 an ounce.

An inventory software vendor's article on wax types calls beeswax "The most expensive wax type by a significant margin." and lists it at "$5-8" a pound (vendor blog, published 2020). That price is from 2020 and below the listing above; use what you pay today. The same supplier listing also says "Beeswax can be very difficult to wick.", so count the test candles you pour in your running costs.

cost per candle = wax oz × price per oz + wick + jar or wrap + label + minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate + overhead

price floor = (cost per candle + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

4. Do not reuse your soy prices

In our 4 oz and 8 oz candle guide, a soy jar priced at $17.00 keeps $7.92. The beeswax jar at the same $17.00 keeps $5.54: $2.38 less on every jar. The difference is the wax: $4.61 of beeswax against $1.43 of soy, 3.22 times as much, less the $0.80 of fragrance the beeswax jar leaves out. A seller-platform article on candle pricing puts it this way: "Beeswax costs almost four times as much per jar. Price it as its own line, well above your soy candles." (seller-platform blog, 30 Sep 2026). Our ratio is lower because the fill weights are invented; weigh your own.

The method for any candle, including how to split a scented pour into wax and fragrance, is in how to price candles, and the soy tin and jar are worked through in how much to sell 4 oz and 8 oz candles for.

6. How many candles (and how much wax) before Christmas

From Mon 26 Oct to Mon 14 Dec 2026 is 49 days, 7 weeks. At 6 making hours a week that is 42 hours: 210 taper pairs at 12 minutes, or 252 pillars at 10 minutes if you have the wax. 210 pairs × 5.60 oz = 1,176.00 oz = 73.50 lb, which is 15 five-pound bags. At the listing's 6+ bag price, 15 × $52.72 = $790.80 of wax to pay for up front. The bulk price saves only $0.10 a pair (5.60 × ($0.6776 − $0.6590)), so buy for the candles you can make, not for the discount.

For delivery by Christmas in the lower 48 states, USPS lists a recommended send-by date of "Thursday, Dec. 17" for Ground Advantage (USPS holiday shipping dates, read Mon 19 Oct 2026). Common questions

How much do beeswax candles sell for?

No source gives a market price, and this guide does not state one. Work from your own cost: in our invented example the floors are $15.92 for a taper pair, $25.75 for a pillar and $16.26 for an 8 oz jar. Then compare with what similar candles sell for near you.

Can you make money selling beeswax candles?

Only if the price carries the wax. In our example a taper pair at $14.00 keeps $3.04, at $18.00 it keeps $6.66 and at $24.00 it keeps $12.09.

Why are beeswax candles more expensive?

The wax costs more per ounce: $0.6776 an ounce from one supplier's 5 lb bag against $0.2311 for the soy in our other candle guide.

Should beeswax candles cost more than soy?

In our example, yes: the same $17.00 keeps $2.38 less on a beeswax jar than on a soy jar.
