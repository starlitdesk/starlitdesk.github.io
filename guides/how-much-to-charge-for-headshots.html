How much to charge for headshots

Charge for headshots from the hours they take, not the minutes in front of the camera. A single headshot is priced as one session; a team headshot day is priced per person, because the setup, travel and admin are shared by everyone in the group. In the worked example below the same photographer needs at least $55.68 per person for a team of 5, but only $30.91 per person for a team of 25, before any profit.

The formula

hours worked = setup and travel + admin + (shoot time + cull and retouch time) × people
cost = cost per hour × hours worked + direct costs
break-even invoice = (cost + fixed card fee) ÷ (1 − card fee %)
floor per person = break-even invoice ÷ people

Your cost per hour comes from a year of costs (your pay, running costs, gear spread over its life) divided by the hours you work on clients. Our guide on how to price a photography session works it out step by step. Your pay is inside that hourly cost, so the break-even price already pays you; profit comes on top.

Step 1: count the hours behind a team day

Illustrative figures only; every input is invented. We use the same invented photographer as the session guide: $40.00 per hour. Time your own team days and use your numbers.

The example team day takes 2.0 hours of travel and setup at the client's office and 1.0 hour of admin (booking, a schedule for staff, invoicing). Each person adds 0.25 hours of shooting and 0.35 hours of culling and retouching, so 0.6 hours per person. Travel costs $30.00. For 15 people that is 2.0 + 1.0 + 0.6 × 15 = 12.0 hours of work.

Step 2: the floor per person

The client pays one invoice by card at an assumed 2.9% + $0.30; use your own provider's rates.

Cost = hours worked × $40.00 + $30.00 travel. Break-even = (cost + $0.30) ÷ 0.971. Floor per person = break-even ÷ people, rounded up to the cent so the group total never falls short.

For 15 people: 12.0 × $40.00 = $480.00, plus $30.00 travel = $510.00. Break-even is $510.30 ÷ 0.971 = $525.54; the card fee on that is $15.54, and $525.54 − $15.54 = $510.00, so the invoice covers the cost and your pay exactly. Split over 15 people, that is $35.04 each. The fixed part of the day (setup, travel, admin) is spread thinner as the group grows, which is why per-person prices usually fall with team size.

Step 3: what tiered per-person prices keep

One photographer in Northeast Ohio lists company headshots at "$95/person" for "4-10 people", "$85/person" for 11-20 and "$75/person" for 21-30, and says to "expect a one-time location fee for on-site sessions to cover travel and studio setup at your office" (Genevieve Nisly Photography, updated 8 Oct 2026). Card fee = invoice × 2.9%, rounded to the cent, + $0.30. You keep = invoice − card fee. Profit over cost = you keep − cost from step 2. Margin = profit ÷ invoice. Pay per hour worked = (you keep − $30.00 travel) ÷ hours worked; it includes the $40.00 an hour already in the cost.

Every tier clears the floor, and the profit grows with the group even though the price per person falls. The small team is the tightest: if the 5-person day took an extra hour of travel, it would cost $310.00 and the $95.00 price would still keep $150.92, but a $60.00 per-person price would not cover it. Check the smallest group you accept against its own floor, or add a location fee, as the rate card above does.

Step 4: a single headshot

A single studio headshot in the example is 2.0 hours of work (booking and a short consult, the shoot, editing and delivery), so it costs $80.00 and breaks even at $80.30 ÷ 0.971 = $82.70. At $150.00 the fee is $4.65, you keep $145.35, which is $65.35 over cost and $72.68 for each hour worked. At $250.00 the fee is $7.55, you keep $242.45, which is $162.45 over cost and $121.23 an hour. For comparison, a New York photographer writes that individual corporate headshots there cost "between $250 and $600 for an individual" and that team days "usually work out to roughly $125–$275 per person with full retouching", because "Setup, travel, and workflow are shared across the whole team" (James Maher Photography, updated July 2026). That is one city's photographer; prices where you work may be very different.

Step 5: put a team day into a pricing sheet

If your calculator has no per-person input, enter the team day as one session: multiply per-person time and price yourself, then type the totals. In the 15-person example that is 0.25 × 15 = 3.75 hours of shooting, 0.35 × 15 = 5.25 hours of culling and retouching, 1.0 hour of admin, 2.0 hours of travel and setup, and a price of $85.00 × 15 = $1,275.00.

Common questions

How much should I charge for headshots per person? At least the floor per person for that group size: the break-even invoice for the whole day divided by the number of people. Smaller groups need a higher price per person, or a location fee.

How much to charge for headshots as a beginner? The same floor applies, because it is your costs and your hours, not your experience, that set it. A beginner may take longer to retouch, so time a few sessions first and use those hours.

Should retouching be included? It is part of the 0.35 hours per person in the example. If you offer light editing at a lower price, cut the hours per person and re-run the sum; charge extra retouching as an add-on priced from its own hours.

Should I charge travel separately? Either way works, as long as it is counted once. In the example travel is inside the cost; a separate location fee would lower the price you need per person.
