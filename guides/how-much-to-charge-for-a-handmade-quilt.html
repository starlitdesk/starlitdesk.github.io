How much should I charge for a handmade quilt?

To price a handmade quilt, add up fabric, batting and thread, add the longarm quilting (or your own quilting hours), pay your time at an hourly rate, add the card fee, then add a margin. In the worked example a throw quilt costs $229.44 in materials and longarm alone, and $629.44 once 20 hours are paid at $20.00. It needs $934.11 for a 30% margin. At a $350.00 price the maker earns about $5.57 an hour.

The short answer: your hours are most of the price

Fabric and batting for a throw cost a little over $100. The quilting adds about as much again. Your own piecing, binding and finishing hours cost more than both together. Three numbers answer the question for your own quilts:

  • True cost: materials, longarm quilting and your time.
  • Break-even price: the price at which a sale pays that cost and the payment fee, and nothing more.
  • Your price: break-even plus the margin you want to keep.

Step 1: know your sizes and yardage

Common finished sizes are Lap/Throw 60" × 72", Twin 60" × 80" and Queen 90" × 108". From 44" fabric, backing takes about 4 to 4.5 yards for a throw, 5 yards for a twin and 6 to 6.5 yards for a queen, and longarm quilters need backing and batting larger than the top, "typically 6–8 inches on all sides" (Linda's backing guide). For the top, a throw needs "about 3 to 4 yards of fabric for the top," plus backing and batting (Mrs Quilty, 12 Feb 2026). Patterns differ, so use the yardage your own pattern gives.

Step 3: longarm quilting cost by size

Longarm services usually charge by the square inch of the quilt top. One guide says "A basic edge-to-edge design usually falls between $0.01 and $0.03 per square inch," with $0.02 the most common, and gives a custom example that "starts at $0.08" (High Country Quilts, 28 Jun 2026). One longarm shop in Georgia lists edge-to-edge at "$0.027 per square inch" with a $50 minimum (shop price page). Quilting it yourself instead? Count those hours. In the example, 6 extra hours at $20.00 = $120.00, about the same as the $116.64 longarm bill (the 6 hours are our guess).

Step 4: pay your time

Time ranges are wide. One quilting blog says a small quilt "can take between 8 to 15 hours total" and a twin about 30 to 50 hours (Mrs Quilty, 13 Feb 2026); another puts a throw at "2-4 weeks" of hobby time (Patchwork Posse). Step 5: break-even and margin price after fees

A card reader charges a percentage of each payment plus a fixed amount. The example uses 2.6% + $0.15 per in-person card payment, the rate Square's US pricing page lists for its Free plan (read Fri 16 Oct 2026). Use your own provider's rate. Adding the margin by dividing, rather than multiplying cost by 2 and 2 again, follows the method in Made Urban's quilting price formula.

break-even price = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %)
price for a target margin = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % − margin %)

Break-even (629.44 + 0.15) ÷ 0.974 = $646.40; for a 30% margin (629.44 + 0.15) ÷ 0.674 = $934.11. Check: the fee on $934.11 is $24.44, which leaves $909.67, or $280.23 over the true cost: 30.00%.

Common questions

How much should I charge for a handmade quilt? At least your break-even price after fees, and usually enough to keep about 30%. In the example that is $646.40 to break even and $934.11 for a 30% margin on a throw.

Why do quilts cost so much? Mostly time. In the example 20 hours at $20.00 is $400.00, more than materials and longarm together ($229.44).

Should a queen cost more than a throw? Yes: it needs more fabric and backing, and longarm cost grows with the area. At $0.027 per square inch a queen is $262.44 against $116.64 for a throw.

Is twice the materials cost a fair price? No. That rule leaves out your hours and the quilting. Use it only as a floor test.
