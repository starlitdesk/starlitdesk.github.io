How much to sell 4 oz and 8 oz candles for

Cost each size on its own: wax, fragrance, wick, vessel, label, your time and overhead, then price so a target margin is left after fees. In our example a 4 oz tin costs $4.74 and needs at least $8.58; an 8 oz jar costs $7.01 and needs $12.33. A small candle is not half a big one: the tin holds less than half the wax but costs about two thirds as much to make, because the vessel, wick, label and your time barely shrink. Halving the jar price undercharges it. This guide works through both sizes side by side.

1. "4 oz" names the vessel, not the wax

Candle sizes are usually named after the container, and the wax inside is less. One candle supplier lists its 4 oz tin with a volume to overflow of "4.70 fl oz" and a wax weight to the fill line of "2.70 oz (76.54 g)", and its 8 oz straight-sided jelly jar at "8.30 fl oz" with "6.20 oz (175.77 g)" of wax to the fill line (supplier listing, tin; supplier listing, jar; read Sun 11 Oct 2026). Weigh what your own vessels actually hold before you cost them: every later number depends on it.

2. Cost each size on its own line

Do not cost one size and scale it. Give each size its own line for wax, fragrance, wick, vessel and lid, label, your minutes and overhead. Wax and fragrance grow with the fill; the other lines barely change. A 10 lb bag of soy container wax listed at $36.98 by one supplier (supplier listing, read Sun 11 Oct 2026; prices change) works out at $36.98 ÷ 160 oz = $0.2311 an ounce.

For how to split a pour into wax and fragrance, and how to set your time and overhead, see the method in how to price candles.

3. Why the small candle costs more per ounce

A tin still needs a wick, a label and nearly as many minutes to pour, cure, label and pack as a jar. Those fixed lines do not halve when the wax does. A seller-platform article on candle pricing puts it plainly: "Small candles tend to cost more per ounce" (seller-platform blog, 30 Sep 2026).

4. Price so your margin survives the fees

Selling fees are usually a percentage of the sale plus a fixed amount per order. To keep the margin you want after fees:

price floor = (cost per candle + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

Check the tin: 2.70 × 0.2311 = $0.62 of wax; 6 minutes at $20.00 an hour = $2.00; all lines together = $4.74. Floor: (4.74 + 0.45) ÷ 0.605 = $8.58. At $10.00 the fees are $1.40, and 10.00 − 1.40 − 4.74 = $3.86, 38.60% of the price.

The tin holds 43.55% of the jar's wax (2.70 ÷ 6.20) but costs 67.62% as much to make (4.74 ÷ 7.01): the vessel, wick, label, time and overhead barely shrink. Per ounce of wax, the tin's floor is $3.18 against the jar's $1.99.

6. The per-ounce trap and the "4x materials" rule

Two shortcuts go wrong on small sizes. The first is to scale from the jar. Half the jar price is $8.50, below the tin's $8.58 floor: fees $1.26, and 8.50 − 1.26 − 4.74 = $2.50, 29.41% of the price. Pricing by the ounce is worse: the same price per ounce of wax as the $17.00 jar is 2.70 × 17.00 ÷ 6.20 = $7.40: fees $1.15, and 7.40 − 1.15 − 4.74 = $1.51, 20.41% of the price.

The second is a materials multiplier. The seller-platform article above suggests prices that cover about four and a half to six and a half times the materials for an 8 oz jar; a simple "4x materials" rule, applied to wax, fragrance, wick, vessel and label in our example:

SizeMaterials× 4FeesCostKept

It works here only because the labour is short. A multiplier ignores your time: on a candle that takes longer to make, the same rule can fall below the floor. Use it as a check, not as the price.

7. Holiday tins and a 3-tin gift set

Tins also work as small gifts and in sets. Price the set from its own cost, box included. Cost: 3 × $4.74 + $1.20 box = $15.42; floor (15.42 + 0.45) ÷ 0.605 = $26.23. Priced at $27.00: fees $3.02, and 27.00 − 3.02 − 15.42 = $8.56, 31.70% of the price. Three single tins bought in one order at $30.00: fees $3.30, cost $14.22, kept $12.48 (41.60%), because each tin is sold at full price; the set trades some margin for a bigger, easier gift. Common questions

How much should I sell a 4 oz candle for? At least its own floor, costed from what the tin really holds. In the example the floor is $8.58 and the tin sells at $10.00. One seller-platform article suggests "$9 to $12 for a 4 ounce candle tin"; check your own floor first.

How much do 4 oz candles sell for? Prices vary widely by wax, fragrance and where you sell. Use other sellers' prices as a check on your floor, not as your cost.

How much should I sell an 8 oz candle for? In the example the floor is $12.33 and the jar sells at $17.00. The same article says "Eight small shops we checked charge a median of $17.50 for an 8 ounce candle", and an inventory software vendor gives a "$16 – $28" range for an 8 oz soy jar (vendor calculator page). These are vendors' figures, not market rates.

How much should I charge for candles? Cost each product on its own, add your time and overhead and price from the floor formula above. The full method is in how to price candles.
