How much to charge for a crochet sweater, size by size

To price a crochet sweater, cost the yarn for that size in whole skeins, count every hour including blocking and packing, pay those hours, then price for fees and margin. Hours are most of it: in our illustrative DK example a size S costs $379.50 to make at $15.00 an hour and needs $505 for a 15% margin; a size XXL needs $685. The same $150 pays $3.64 an hour on the S and $1.87 on the XXL.

1. Why a sweater costs more than people expect

A sweater is the biggest time job most crocheters sell. One maker writes: "The number that comes to mind personally is around 20 hours for finishing a sweater." In the same blog's informal survey, "44% of people surveyed answered it would take them 30 hours or more", and the post sums up: "You are looking at a time committment of 20-30 hours over a few weeks to a month." (one crochet blog's survey, published 21 Mar 2022, updated 13 Jul 2022). That is one maker's 2022 survey, not a standard; time your own sweater from first chain to packed.

At any fair hourly rate, 20 or more hours is far more money than the yarn. That is why a price that feels right for a scarf does not work for a sweater. The general method is in how to price crochet and knitted items; this guide applies it size by size.

2. Yarn by size: why bigger sizes need a bigger price

Patterns list yarn for each size, and the larger sizes need much more. One DK pullover pattern lists "1750 (1860, 1970, 2300, 2405) yards" for sizes "S, M, L, XL, XXL" (pattern page, first published December 2023). A sport-weight pattern lists "1804 - 3664 yards (1650 - 3350 m)" across nine sizes from XS to 5XL (pattern page, first published March 2024): its largest size needs about twice the yarn of its smallest.

More yarn means more rows, so more hours. Price each size from its own yarn and hours, and cost the yarn in the whole skeins you buy for it.

Fees = price × 9.5%, rounded to the cent, + $0.45. Profit = price − fees − full cost. Per hour worked = (profit + labour) ÷ hours.

For size M: (406.25 + 0.45) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.15) = $538.68 for a 15% margin, so $540.00. The XXL costs $136.50 more to make than the S, and its price is $180 higher. If you charge one flat price for every size at the M price, $540, an XXL makes a loss of $27.75 and pays $14.04 an hour, not $15.00.

Yarn is a small share: materials for size M are $65.00, 12.04% of the $540 price. The hours are the price.

Fees at $150 are $14.70; at $250, $24.20. Size S at $150: (150.00 − 14.70 − 57.00) ÷ 21.50 = $3.64.

In this example a $150 sweater pays under $4 an hour in every size, and even $250 pays under $8. Which price to charge is your call; the table shows what each one pays. If the full-cost price is more than your buyers pay, the honest options are a simpler stitch, heavier yarn, a shorter or cropped style, or taking sweaters only as priced commissions.

5. Taking a sweater commission: yarn money up front

A commission ties up weeks of your time and yarn bought for one person's size and colour. In the example the size M yarn is $64.00 (8 skeins × $8.00), 11.85% of the $540 price. If you ask for the yarn money before you buy it, you are not left holding yarn bought for one order; see how much deposit to take on a custom order for sizing a deposit. This guide does not cover contracts or refunds.

6. How many sweaters before Christmas

Count your hours before you say yes. From Mon 26 Oct to Mon 14 Dec 2026 is 49 days, 7 weeks. At 8 crochet hours a week that is 56.00 hours: 2 sweaters in any size from S to XL (two size M sweaters leave 10.50 hours spare), or 1 XXL (27.00 hours spare). For delivery by Christmas in the lower 48 states, USPS lists a recommended send-by date of "Thursday, Dec. 17" for Ground Advantage (USPS holiday shipping dates, read Mon 19 Oct 2026). Common questions

How much should I sell a crochet sweater for? At least your floor from yarn, hours and fees; no source gives a market price, and this guide does not state one. In our example that is $505 for a size S and $685 for an XXL at $15.00 an hour.

How much to charge for a crochet cardigan? Use the same method. Add buttons to materials, and the bands and button holes to your hours.

How much to charge per hour for crochet? Your rate is your choice. A pricing article on a website-builder's blog writes: "Your hourly rate should reflect your skill and experience while also producing a final price that works for your business." (website-builder blog, 17 Oct 2026). The example uses $15.00, as our other crochet guides do; see how to work out your hourly rate.

Why is a handmade crochet sweater so expensive? Time. In the blog survey above, 44% said a sweater would take them 30 hours or more; at $15.00 an hour, 20 hours is $300.00 before yarn or fees.

Selling a sweater made from someone else's pattern? Read that pattern's terms for selling finished items first. Pricing a scarf or a blanket instead? See how much to charge for a crochet scarf and how much to charge for a crochet blanket.
