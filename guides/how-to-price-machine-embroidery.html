How to price machine embroidery: stitch count to minutes, blanks, thread and your fees

Many machine embroiderers quote a rate per 1,000 stitches with a minimum charge. It is quick, and customers understand it. But a stitch rate prices the stitching, not the piece: the blank, the stabilizer, the colour changes, the hooping and the selling fees all have to fit inside it. This guide shows how to turn a design's stitch count into machine minutes and thread cost, add the blank and your time, and price the finished piece so your margin survives the fees. Then it compares the result with a stitch-rate quote.

1. Turn stitch count into minutes

Your digitizing software gives the stitch count and the number of colours. Divide the stitches by your machine's real average speed (not the top speed on the box: thread breaks, trims and slow sections pull it down), then add time for each colour change. Hooping and unhooping come on top, piece by piece.

run minutes = stitches ÷ stitches per minute + colours × minutes per colour change
labour minutes = hooping minutes + run minutes

If you run several heads, or do other work while the machine stitches, you may decide to count only part of the run time as your labour. Whatever you choose, use the same rule for every design.

2. Cost the thread by the 1,000 stitches

Top thread and bobbin are used in proportion to stitches. Work out a cost per 1,000 stitches once from what a cone and a bobbin last you, and reuse it.

thread cost = stitches ÷ 1,000 × thread and bobbin cost per 1,000

3. Add the blank and everything else that goes into the piece

The blank (sweatshirt, tote, cap, fabric) is often the biggest cost. Add the stabilizer or backing, and anything else the piece leaves with: a hoop frame, a gift bag, packaging.

material cost = blank + stabilizer + other + thread cost

4. Add time and overhead, then price for fees

Pay yourself for the labour minutes at an hourly rate you would accept, and add a small overhead allowance per piece for needles, machine servicing and software. Selling fees are usually a percentage of the sale plus a fixed amount per order. To keep the margin you want after fees:

price = (cost per piece + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

How a stitch-rate quote compares

The stitch rate itself is not wrong; in this example it is simply too low for a finished piece sold with fees. It leaves out the stabilizer, the frame, the colour changes and the hooping, and the fees come off the whole price, blank included. A stitch rate suits decorating a customer's own garment. For finished pieces you sell, start from cost and use the stitch rate as a check.

What leaving out colour changes and hooping does

Count only stitches ÷ speed as your time and the costs drop to $20.14, $7.17 and $17.87. Priced at the rounded results, $35, $13 and $31, the pieces really keep $9.59 (27%), $3.40 (26%) and $7.64 (25%), not 30%. The more colours a design has, the more this shortcut costs you.

5. Re-check when something changes

Re-run the numbers when blank prices change, when you switch thread, when a new design has many colours or a high stitch count, and once you have timed a few real runs. Your measured speed is worth more than any rule of thumb.
