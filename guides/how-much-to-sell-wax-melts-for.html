How much should I sell wax melts for?

Cost one clamshell (wax and fragrance as your recipe costs them, the clamshell, label, seal, your minutes and overhead), then price so a margin is left after fees. In our example a 3 oz clamshell costs $2.60, breaks even at $3.37 and needs $5.04 for a 30% margin. At $5.00 a single melt keeps $1.47; a 3 for $15 order keeps $5.32, because the fixed fee per order is paid once.

The short answer: cost, floor, then bundles

Wax melts are cheap to make and cheap to buy, which is why they sell well as stocking fillers and small gifts. It is also why the pricing is tight: on a $5 item, a fixed fee per order and a few cents of packaging decide whether you make anything. One craft-fair guide suggests "$5 or $6 per clamshell as the single-unit price." and bundles of "3 for $15 as the introductory bundle." and "5 for $25 as the volume bundle." (TheCraftMap, 5 May 2026). Those are one guide's figures, not your price. Three numbers set yours:

  • Cost per clamshell: wax, fragrance, clamshell, label, seal, your time and overhead.
  • Break-even and floor: the price that pays cost and fees, and the price that also leaves your target margin.
  • The order size: the fixed fee is paid per order, so a bundle keeps more per melt than single sales.

Step 1: cost one clamshell

The same guide gives "Clamshell six-cubes (2.5 to 3 oz)." as a standard size, and says "Materials for a 2.5 ounce clamshell typically run $0.80 to $1.50 once you're buying soy wax and fragrance oil in bulk." For packaging it gives labels at "about $0.10 to $0.20 per clamshell." and shrink bands at "about $0.15 per unit." One supplier listing says its 6-cavity clamshell "holds 3 oz of wax" and sells a "50 pack - $26.60" (read Tue 13 Oct 2026; prices change).

Step 2: break-even and the 30% floor

break-even = (cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee %)
floor = (cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

Break-even: (2.60 + 0.45) ÷ 0.905 = $3.37. Check: fees on $3.37 are $0.32 + $0.45 = $0.77, and 3.37 − 0.77 − 2.60 = $0.00. Floor at a 30% margin: (2.60 + 0.45) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = $5.04.

Fees = order × 9.5%, rounded to the cent, + $0.45. Kept = price − cost − fees.

$5.00 is just under the $5.04 floor: a 29.40% margin. At $4.00 the $0.45 order fee alone is 11.25% of the price, and the melt keeps $0.57. Per ounce, $6.00 for 3 oz is $2.00 an ounce.

A bundle at the single price per melt is not a discount for you: three separate $5.00 orders keep 3 × $1.47 = $4.41, while one 3 for $15 order keeps $5.32, because the $0.45 order fee is paid once instead of three times. If the bundle costs you more packing time or a bag, add it to the cost line first.

Step 5: a 3-melt holiday gift box

The guide also suggests "$15 to $25 for gift sets." and notes "These three together make a great hostess gift." Price a gift box from its own cost: 3 × $2.60 + $1.10 kraft box (assumed) = $8.90. Floor: (8.90 + 0.45) ÷ 0.605 = $15.45.

Common questions

How much should I sell wax melts for? At least your floor: $5.04 per 3 oz clamshell in our example. Cost your own recipe and packaging first.

How much do people sell wax melts for? One craft-fair guide suggests $5 or $6 a clamshell, with bundles of 3 for $15 and 5 for $25. Use other prices as a check on your floor, not as your cost.

Is 3 for $15 a good deal for me? In the example, yes: it keeps $5.32 against $4.41 for three single $5.00 orders, because one order fee is paid instead of three.

How should I price a wax melt gift set? From its own cost, box included: $8.90 in the example, with a floor of $15.45. Candles in tins and jars? See how much to sell 4 oz and 8 oz candles for.
