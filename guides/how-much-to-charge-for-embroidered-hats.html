How much to charge for embroidered hats

Start from the blank cap, then cost the stitching at cap speed, not flat speed: caps are usually run at around 600 stitches a minute or slower. Add hooping, thread, backing, foam for 3D puff, your time and overhead, and price so your margin is left after fees. In our example a 5,000-stitch flat-embroidered cap costs $15.64 and sells at $27.00; the same design in 3D puff costs $19.06 and needs $33.00. Caps take longer than flat pieces at every step, so a price copied from flat work, or a blank plus a stitch charge, leaves too little; this guide shows the full cost stack with a worked example.

1. Start with the blank cap

The blank is usually the biggest single cost. Use what you actually pay per cap, including shipping and any caps you lose to mistakes. Structured caps with a stiff front panel are the easiest to stitch; unstructured and low-profile caps can need more backing and care. Keep the design at least half an inch from the bill and the sides, as one machine dealer's guide advises (dealer blog).

2. Cost stitches at cap speed, not flat speed

Caps sit on a curved frame, and machines are run slower on them. One machine dealer writes that "for a typical hat design, a machine should run at about 600 stitches per minute with a hat frame and around 750 for a flat", and about 550 on a hat frame for intricate designs (dealer blog). A digitizing service puts "the typical and efficient speed of cap embroidery" at 600 stitches a minute, and structured caps at 500 to 600 (digitizing service). These are vendors' rules of thumb; time a few of your own caps.

run minutes = stitches ÷ cap speed + colours × minutes per colour change

3. Hooping and colour changes take longer on caps

Each cap has to be hooped on the cap frame, checked for straightness and unhooped, one by one. Count those minutes as labour on top of the run time. Colour changes add up the same way as on flat work.

labour minutes = hooping minutes + run minutes

4. 3D puff: foam, more stitches, a slower run

3D puff stitches over a layer of foam to raise the design. A machine maker recommends "running your machine at 500 to 600 stitches per minute" for puff on caps, slower than usual, and leaving foam around the design (machine maker's blog). Puff digitizing needs "longer stitches to account for the space the foam occupies" (ASI Central), so a puff version of a design usually has more stitches. How many more depends on the design; the example adds 50%, which is our guess, not a rule.

Cost the foam by the piece you cut. One supplier lists 3 mm embroidery foam at $3.86 for an 11.5 × 18 inch sheet (supplier listing, read Sun 11 Oct 2026; prices change). A 4 × 3 inch piece is 12 square inches of a 207 square inch sheet: 12 ÷ 207 × $3.86 = $0.22. Add a few minutes for cutting the foam and tearing away the excess.

5. Price so your margin survives the fees

Selling fees are usually a percentage of the sale plus a fixed amount per order. To keep the margin you want after fees:

price floor = (cost per cap + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin %)

The puff cap costs $3.42 more to make and is priced $6.00 higher, so the puff cap keeps a similar margin. Price puff as its own product, not as a small extra.

A 12-cap gift order. 12 × $27.00 = $324.00. One order means one fixed fee: fees $31.23. Cost 12 × $15.64 = $187.68. Kept: 324.00 − 31.23 − 187.68 = $105.09, 32.44% of the order, against $100.08 for 12 separate orders, because the fixed part of the fee is paid once. Hooping time still counts for every cap. 7. The flat-speed mistake

Cost the same flat cap as if it were a flat piece at 700 stitches a minute, with no hooping or colour changes: 5,000 ÷ 700 = 7.14 minutes, $2.14 of labour, a cost of $13.63 and a floor of $23.27, so the cap goes out at $24.00. Its real cost is still $15.64: fees $2.73, and 24.00 − 2.73 − 15.64 = $5.63, 23.46% of the price instead of 30%.

8. Why "blank + stitch charge" undercharges a finished cap

Decorators often quote a charge for the stitching on top of the blank, for example $10.00 for the first 5,000 stitches and $2.00 for each extra 1,000 (an example rate). Applied to a finished cap you sell, with the full cost and fees:

Common questions

How much to charge for embroidered hats? At least your floor: cost per cap plus the fixed fee, divided by what is left after the fee % and your target margin. In the example that is $26.60 for the flat cap and $32.25 for the puff cap.

How much to charge for custom hats? The same method, with the customer's design: get the stitch count and colours from the digitized file, cost it at cap speed and add any digitizing time you spend.

How much does it cost to have a hat embroidered? From the maker's side, it is the stack in the table: blank, backing, thread, foam for puff, hooping and run time, overhead and fees. That is why cap prices vary so much between sellers.

How much should I charge for embroidery in general? For sweatshirts, totes and hoop art, see how to price machine embroidery.

When should I stop taking cap orders for Christmas? Count back from the delivery date. As an example, a cap order that has to arrive by Christmas Day, Fri 25 Dec, needs to be stitched, packed and posted well before; three weeks ahead is Fri 4 Dec. See when to stop taking custom Christmas orders.
