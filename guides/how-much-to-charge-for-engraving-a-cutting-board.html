How much should I charge to engrave a cutting board?

Add the blank, the oil and box, every minute you spend (proof, setup, engraving, oiling, packing) at your hourly rate, a little machine time and running costs, then price for a margin after fees. In our example one personalised board costs $16.54, breaks even at $18.55 and keeps $10.36 (34.53%) at $30.00.

The short answer: blank, minutes, fees, then margin

Engraved cutting boards are a popular gift for Christmas, hosts and new homes, and quoted prices are wide. One laser maker's blog says that for professional engraving you should "expect to pay somewhere between $30 and $100 a board." (LaserPecker blog, 10 Apr 2026). Another laser seller lists cutting board prices as "Higher for custom layouts or large engravings." (Thunder Laser USA, 4 Feb 2026). A range cannot tell you where your price should sit. Your costs can:

  • True cost: blank, oil and box, your minutes, machine time and a share of running costs.
  • Break-even price: the price at which a sale pays that cost and the selling fees.
  • Your price: break-even plus the margin you want to keep.

Step 1: cost one board

A board has a large engraving area, so the laser runs longer than on a tumbler or a coaster, and you still prepare a proof for each name. The example counts every minute you are at the machine at the hourly rate from how to work out your hourly rate. The machine rate of $0.865 an hour is the example rate from how to price laser engraving; we do not repeat how it is worked out here.

Step 2: break-even and a 30% margin price

The example uses an invented fee of 9.5% of the order plus $0.25 per order, the same example rate as our tumbler guide; use your own.

break-even price = (true cost + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee %)
price for a target margin = (true cost + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % − margin %)

Break-even: (16.54 + 0.25) ÷ 0.905 = $18.55. Check: fees on $18.55 are $1.76 + $0.25 = $2.01, and 18.55 − 2.01 − 16.54 = $0.00. For a 30% margin: (16.54 + 0.25) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = $27.75, so $30.00 is a reasonable single price.

At $20.00 the board only just pays your time: each hour earns $22.62 against the $20.00 you set, a 6.55% margin. At $30.00 it earns $40.72.

Step 4: the customer brings the board

When a customer supplies the board, you sell only your time: 27 minutes (10 proof, 3 setup, 12 engraving, 2 cleaning; no oil or box) = $9.00 labour + $0.17 machine + $0.50 running costs = $9.67.

Rules of thumb you will see

  • Setup fee plus a run charge: trade suppliers bill setup once and a charge per piece. One promotional-products supplier lists laser engraving on bamboo boards at "Setup $60.00" plus a "Run Charge $1.25/ea", with a minimum of 100 units (Promotional Product Inc., read 13 Oct 2026). The same idea works for a small maker: charge the proof and setup once per design, then a price per board.
  • Setup fees for one-off jobs: Thunder Laser USA says setup fees are charged "especially for custom jobs" and cover file preparation, test runs and machine setup. On a single board those 13 minutes are $4.33 of the $10.00 labour in our example.
  • Quoted ranges: $30 to $100 a board is a range for comparison, not your price. In the example $30.00 keeps 34.53%; a dearer blank or a longer engraving moves every figure.

Common questions

How much does it cost to engrave a cutting board? In our example a personalised board costs $16.54 to make, including your time, and breaks even at $18.55 after fees. Quoted prices run from about $30 to $100.

How much should I charge for engraving? At least break-even after fees, and usually enough for a margin of around 30% on top of paying your time: $30.00 in the example.

Should I charge a setup fee for one board? Count the proof and setup minutes either way. On one board they are part of the price; on a matching order, charge them once.

What if a customer brings their own board? Charge for your time, machine and running costs plus margin: $15.00 keeps $3.65 in the example, and $10.00 loses money. Agree who replaces a spoiled board first.
