Guides for handmade sellers

Practical guides to costing and pricing handmade work, and to running your workshop, with worked examples you can redo with your own numbers.

  • How to price handmade items: a simple formula that includes your time and selling fees
    A simple formula for pricing handmade items: materials, your time, packaging and overhead, priced so your margin survives selling fees. Worked example and guides for 10 crafts.
  • How to price candles: a cost-per-candle method that includes your time and selling fees
    A step-by-step method to cost one candle from a whole pour, add your time and overhead, and set a price that keeps your margin after selling fees. Worked example included.
  • How to price crochet and knitted items: find what you really earn per hour
    Cost yarn by weight, time your work honestly and see what you really earn per hour after fees, then decide what to do about the gap. Worked example included.
  • How to price cookies and baked goods: cost per cookie and per box, with your time and fees
    Turn pack prices into cost per gram, cost a whole batch after breakage, add oven, packaging and your time, and price a box of 12 that keeps your margin after fees. Worked example included.
  • How to price 3D prints: filament, machine time and failed prints, not just grams
    Cost filament per gram, turn your printer into a cost per print hour, spread failed prints over the good ones, add your time and price so your margin survives the fees. Worked example included.
  • How to price handmade soap: cost per sellable bar, with trim, your time and selling fees
    Cost a whole batch from your recipe, divide by the bars you can actually sell after trim and rejects, add wrapping and your time, and set a price that keeps your margin after fees. Worked example included.
  • How to price jewelry when metal prices move: cost per piece, a re-price trigger and your fees
    Cost each piece from the metal you actually use, including workshop loss, add findings, packaging and your time, price for your margin after fees, and work out the metal price at which each piece needs a new price. Worked example included.
  • How to price pottery: kiln firing cost per piece, cracked pieces, your time and fees
    Work out what a kiln load costs and split it per piece by shelf space, spread cracks and seconds over the pieces you sell, add your time and price so your margin survives the fees. Worked example included.
  • How to price machine embroidery: stitch count to minutes, blanks, thread and your fees
    Turn a design's stitch count and colour changes into machine minutes and thread cost, add the blank, stabilizer and your time, price the finished piece for your margin after fees, and compare it with a per-1,000-stitch quote. Worked example included.
  • How to price laser engraving: machine cost per minute, pieces per sheet and setup time
    Turn your machine into a cost per minute, count pieces per sheet, spread setup over the batch, add your time and price so your margin survives the fees, including what one personalised piece really costs. Worked example included.
  • How to price flower arrangements: cost per stem, waste, your time and where markup falls short
    Turn bunch prices into a cost per stem, cost each design from its recipe with waste and hardgoods, add your time and price so your margin survives the fees, then compare with a classic florist markup. Worked example included.
  • When to stop taking custom Christmas orders: work back from the last shipping date
    Find your cut-off for custom Christmas orders: start from the carrier's last send date, take off packing, a buffer and work already booked. Worked example included.
  • How much inventory to bring to a craft fair: sales target, sell-through and break-even
    Work out how many items to bring to a craft fair from your sales target, average price and sell-through, split it by product and check break-even. Worked example included.
  • Is a craft fair worth it? Booth fee, profit per hour and a slow-day test
    Work out whether a craft fair pays: list every event cost, count only the stock you sell, divide profit by every hour the fair takes, find break-even sales and test a slow day. Two example fairs compared.
  • How to work out your hourly rate for handmade work: pay for the hours you are not making
    Divide the yearly pay you want by your making hours, not all your working hours, so admin, photos and packing are paid too. Check your current prices against the rate. Worked example included.
