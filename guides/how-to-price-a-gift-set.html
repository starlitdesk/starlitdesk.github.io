How do I price a gift set without losing money?

To price a gift set, add up the items' full prices, take off a modest saving (10-20% is the usual range), then subtract the item costs, the gift packaging and the selling fees. If what is left beats the margin on the one item a gift buyer would otherwise take, the set earns its place. In our worked example, three $8.00 soaps sold as a $20.00 set keep $7.35: $3.36 more than one soap sold alone, but $5.12 less than the same three soaps sold at full price in one order. A set pays when it turns one-item buyers into set buyers, not when it goes to people who wanted all three anyway.

The short answer: full prices, a modest saving, then a margin check

Most advice on gift sets stops at "take 10-20% off". That sets a price, but it does not tell you whether the set makes you money. Two comparisons do:

  • Against a single sale: does the set earn more than the one item a gift buyer would have bought instead? If yes, every buyer you move up to the set is extra profit.
  • Against a full-price order: how much does each set give up when it goes to someone who would have bought all the items anyway? That is the saving plus the gift box.

A consultancy that models bundles for online shops puts the test this way: "A bundle only improves profit if its contribution dollars per order hold at or above the same items sold separately" (Eightx). For a handmade gift set, the single-item comparison matters just as much, because many gift buyers came for one thing.

Step 1: add up the full prices and the true cost of each item

Start from the prices you already charge and the true cost of each item: materials, the item's own packaging, your time and a share of overhead. If you have not costed your items yet, the method is in how to price handmade items, and how to work out your hourly rate sets the labour line. A set is never cheaper to make than its items; it only shares one order and one round of packing.

Step 2: add the gift packaging as a cost of the set

The box, tissue, ribbon and tag are a real cost, and packaging belongs in your base cost like materials (Small Business Trends). One vendor guide gives these ranges per set: kraft boxes with tissue and a sticker seal $0.50-1.00, cellophane bags with a ribbon $0.25-0.50, muslin drawstring bags with a printed tag $0.75-1.50, and small baskets or trays $2-5 (TheCraftMap). Price your own from the pack you buy, and add the minutes it takes to assemble and wrap a set if they are not small.

Step 3: pick the saving

The same guide's rule of thumb: add up the individual prices and take 10-20% off, and never more than 20% off the combined price; its example is three soaps at $8 ($24) sold as a $20 bundle. It also suggests clean, round prices and puts the sweet spot for craft fair gift sets at $20-40, with most impulse buyers lost above $50 (TheCraftMap). These are rules of thumb from one vendor, not research, so treat them as a starting point and check the margin in step 4.

Step 4: check the set against a single sale and a full-price order

Illustrative figures only, not the figures from our soap guide. Each soap sells for $8.00 and has a true cost of $3.00 (materials, its own wrap and time), so three cost $9.00. The gift box with ribbon is $1.50 per set (invented). Selling fees are an invented example of 9.5% of the sale price plus $0.25 per order, not any platform's rate: use your own.

First the two benchmarks. One soap sold alone: fees are $0.76 + $0.25 = $1.01, so it keeps 8.00 − 3.00 − 1.01 = $3.99. Three soaps in one full-price order, with no gift box: fees are $2.28 + $0.25 = $2.53, so the order keeps 24.00 − 9.00 − 2.53 = $12.47.

Even with no saving at all, the set is $1.50 behind the full-price order: that is the box. Every step of saving widens the gap, while the lead over a single sale shrinks. At the round $20.00 price, the set keeps $7.35, which is $3.36 more than a single soap and $5.12 less than three soaps sold at full price.

Your set's floor price

The floor price is the lowest set price that still pays the items, the box and the fees:

floor price = (item costs + gift packaging + fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − fee %)

In the example: (9.00 + 1.50 + 0.25) ÷ 0.905 = $11.88. Check: fees on $11.88 are $1.13 + $0.25 = $1.38, and 11.88 − 9.00 − 1.50 − 1.38 = $0.00. That is a saving of 50.50% on $24.00, so the usual 10-20% sits far above the floor here. Because your time is inside the true cost, the floor still pays you; it leaves nothing extra, so treat it as a limit, not a price.

Reading the result

The $20.00 set is worth offering to someone who came for one soap: each of those buyers adds $3.36. If most set buyers would have bought three soaps anyway, each set costs you $5.12. You cannot know in advance which happens, so this is our framing, not a sourced claim: after a market or a month online, look at whether your set sales came with fewer single sales or fewer full multi-item orders, and adjust the saving.

A set also ships as one parcel, which can make postage per item cheaper than separate orders; work out the charge with how much to charge for shipping. And if sets are part of your Christmas plan, count them in how much holiday stock to make.

Common questions

How much should I discount a craft fair bundle compared to individual prices? 10-20% off the combined price is the usual rule of thumb, capped at 20%. Check the margin per set against a single sale and against a full-price order before you settle on it.

What's the best price range for craft fair gift sets? One vendor guide puts the sweet spot at $20-40. Whatever the range, round to a clean price and confirm it is above your floor.

Should I let customers customize their own bundles? That is your call. In maths terms, each combination has its own item costs, so run the same check for the cheapest and the most expensive mix.

What is bundle pricing for product businesses? Selling several items together for one price, usually below the sum of their separate prices. It works when the set adds sales you would not otherwise make.

How do I offer gift set discounts? Price the set from full prices less a modest saving, add the gift packaging as a cost, and check the margin with the two comparisons above.
